# 1.8.1 review: the blind back-translations

`lj.warn.rewardsMoved` (critical since chunk 43, #367) in Spanish and Japanese, back-translated by a fresh agent a language (an Explore agent, given only the text below and where it shows: no English, no key names, no files), in two rounds: round one's three frames, then the five that worker#1's rethrow put it in. English: *The network refused it: Lovejoin's pool changed as it was sent. Nothing was lost.*

**Verdict:** every clause held in both languages and both rounds; nothing drifted. The notes are about what the English leaves open too (its "it", "as it was sent") or about older frames, so nothing changed. Both verified files' reviewer notes record it.

## Round one: alone, after Why it stopped, and in the chains' line

### Spanish

1. La red lo rechazó: el pool de Lovejoin cambió mientras se enviaba. No se perdió nada.
   - **Back:** The network rejected it: the Lovejoin pool changed while it was being sent. Nothing was lost.
   - **Notes:** 'lo' (it) has no antecedent when the message is shown on its own, so the reader has to guess what was rejected. It is also masculine/neuter, so it cannot grammatically stand for a feminine noun like 'la transacción' (that would be 'la rechazó'). 'mientras se enviaba' has no stated subject. The nearest noun is 'el pool', so it can literally mean the pool was being sent. The intended subject is presumably whatever was rejected, and English 'it' keeps the same ambiguity. 'La red' can mean the Cardano network or the user's internet connection, so 'the network rejected it' could be taken as a connection problem. 'cambió' does not say how the pool changed: its boxes moved on, or the pool itself was altered. 'No se perdió nada' does not say what was not lost (funds, fees, progress). Under a button that brings funds back, 'nothing was lost' may make a reader wonder where the funds are and why they need bringing back. 'pool' is an English loanword, and in Cardano Spanish it usually suggests a stake pool.

2. Por qué se detuvo: La red lo rechazó: el pool de Lovejoin cambió mientras se enviaba. No se perdió nada.
   - **Back:** Why it stopped: The network rejected it: the Lovejoin pool changed while it was being sent. Nothing was lost.
   - **Notes:** Everything noted for 1 applies: 'lo' and 'se enviaba' have no stated subject, 'la red' can mean the blockchain or the connection, 'cambió' is vague, and 'nada' does not say what. New here: after 'Por qué se detuvo', a reader may take 'lo' to be the series that stopped, so the network rejected the whole series rather than one transaction in it. In the same way, 'mientras se enviaba' can mean at any point while the series was going out, or while the one failing transaction was being sent. In a series that stopped partway, 'No se perdió nada' can mean no funds were lost or no progress was lost. Two colons in one line is awkward. The capital 'La' after the first colon (label style) and the lowercase 'el' after the second are inconsistent; I kept both as written. 'se detuvo' most naturally means 'it stopped', but it can also be read as passive: 'it was stopped'.

3. Por qué se detuvo: La red lo rechazó: el pool de Lovejoin cambió mientras se enviaba. No se perdió nada. Mezclar mis cajas otra vez mezcla las cajas que no mezcló.
   - **Back:** Why it stopped: The network rejected it: the Lovejoin pool changed while it was being sent. Nothing was lost. Mix my boxes again mixes the boxes that it didn't mix.
   - **Notes:** Everything noted for 2 applies. 'que no mezcló' has no stated subject, so it can be read three ways: the series ('it didn't mix'), formal usted ('you didn't mix'), or, because the button name is the subject of the sentence, the button itself ('Mix my boxes again mixes the boxes it didn't mix'), which is circular. English 'it' keeps the series and button readings but loses the 'you' one. The button name is the subject but has no quotes or formatting, so at first it can read as an ordinary infinitive phrase ('Mixing my boxes again mixes...'), and then 'mis' (my) sounds odd coming from the app. I left it unquoted, as in the Spanish. 'mezcla' has the same form as the tú imperative ('mix the boxes...'): a possible but unlikely misreading. 'mezcla las cajas que no mezcló' repeats the verb and is clunky. 'otra vez' in the button name suggests mixing boxes again, including ones already mixed, but the sentence says it mixes only the unmixed ones; 'again' can mean 'retry' or 're-mix'. The scope is unclear too: the line belongs to one series in a list but the button is for the whole page. The singular 'no mezcló' points to this series' leftover boxes only, so it is not clear whether the button mixes just those or the leftovers of every series listed.

### Japanese

1. 送信中に Lovejoin のプールが変わったため、ネットワークが拒否しました。失われたものはありません。
   - **Back:** The Lovejoin pool changed during sending, so the network rejected it. Nothing has been lost.
   - **Notes:** 送信中に names no subject or object: it doesn't say what was being sent or by whom. It most naturally goes with 変わった (the pool changed during sending), but can also be read with 拒否しました (the network rejected it during sending, because the pool had changed). プールが変わった is vague and has no agent: it can mean the pool's contents changed (for example, boxes in it were used by someone else) or that the pool itself was swapped or replaced. To Cardano users プール can also suggest a stake pool, though 'Lovejoin の' narrows it. ネットワークが拒否しました drops its object, so what was rejected comes only from where the message sits (hence 'it' in English). Under the button that brings funds back, it can read as that button's action having been refused; in place of a sent confirmation, as the send having been refused. 送信 ('transmit') plus ネットワーク could also be taken as a connection problem rather than the Cardano network rejecting a transaction. The active form is grammatical, but a passive with the rejected thing as subject (〜がネットワークに拒否されました) is more usual in UI text. 失われたものはありません is literally 'there is nothing that was lost'; the present main verb over a past relative clause is why I used 'has been lost'. It doesn't say what it covers (funds, fees, boxes). Next to a button that brings funds back, it reassures, but it could also seem at odds with needing to bring funds back. It's slightly stiff; 何も失われていません would be more usual.

2. 停止した理由: 送信中に Lovejoin のプールが変わったため、ネットワークが拒否しました。失われたものはありません。
   - **Back:** Reason it stopped: The Lovejoin pool changed during sending, so the network rejected it. Nothing has been lost.
   - **Notes:** The shared sentence has the same points as 1. 停止した理由 leaves out what stopped (the series). 停止する is both intransitive and transitive, so it can read 'the reason it stopped' or 'the reason (the wallet) stopped it'. 停止 also doesn't say whether this is final or a pause that can be resumed. The label gives the reason for stopping, but the sentence explains a rejection; that the rejection is what stopped the series is only implied. In a series, 送信中に doesn't say which send it means (presumably the one where it stopped). In English the two 'it's ('it stopped', 'rejected it') may look like the same thing; the Japanese states neither, and the rejected thing is presumably one transaction, not the whole series. 失われたものはありません has no stated scope in a series that stopped partway: it can mean no funds were lost, or that the steps finished before the stop still stand.

3. 停止した理由: 送信中に Lovejoin のプールが変わったため、ネットワークが拒否しました。失われたものはありません。ミックスされなかったボックスは「ボックスを再ミックス」でミックスできます。
   - **Back:** Reason it stopped: The Lovejoin pool changed during sending, so the network rejected it. Nothing has been lost. Boxes that were not mixed can be mixed using “Remix boxes”.
   - **Notes:** The shared sentences have the same points as 1 and 2. ミックスされなかったボックス doesn't say whether it means this series' boxes left unmixed when it stopped, or any unmixed boxes. It doesn't mark number either (one box or several). 'Boxes' without 'the' keeps that open; 'The boxes' would commit to this series. The button's 再 ('re-', 'again') sits oddly with boxes that 'were not mixed'. Here it has to mean 'retry the mix', but the label on its own suggests mixing again boxes that were already mixed, so a reader may wonder whether the button works on never-mixed boxes. The line is in a list of series, but the button is a single one on the page; the text doesn't say whether it acts on this series' boxes only or on all of them. ミックス three times in the last sentence is a little repetitive. で means 'by means of' (the button); English 'mixed with' would suggest 'mixed together with', so I used 'using'. 'Remix boxes' is my rendering of the label; if the English UI already names this button, use that label.

## Round two: the runner's detail, Bring everything back's row, a swap's retry, Bring every box back's lines

### Spanish

1. Por qué se detuvo: La red lo rechazó: el pool de Lovejoin cambió mientras se enviaba. No se perdió nada. Lo intenta otra vez a las 04:00:00. Qué salió mal: La red lo rechazó: el pool de Lovejoin cambió mientras se enviaba. No se perdió nada.
   - **Back:** Why it stopped: The network rejected it: the Lovejoin pool changed while it was being sent. Nothing was lost. It tries again at 04:00:00. What went wrong: The network rejected it: the Lovejoin pool changed while it was being sent. Nothing was lost.
   - **Notes:** (a) No subject is ever named. 'se detuvo' (it stopped) presumably refers to the series. But 'La red lo rechazó' uses masculine 'lo', which can't stand for 'la serie' or 'la transacción' (both feminine). It points to some unnamed masculine thing ('envío', 'intento', 'paso'?). English turns both into 'it', so the same thing seems to have stopped and been rejected. The Spanish hints they are different things but names neither. (b) 'el pool de Lovejoin cambió mientras se enviaba': 'se enviaba' has no subject. The default grammatical reading carries over the subject of 'cambió', so it says the pool itself was being sent. The intended reading is that the rejected item was being sent. English 'while it was being sent' has the same two readings. (c) 'Lo intenta otra vez a las 04:00:00' uses the present tense for a scheduled future. I kept the present ('It tries again'); the meaning is 'It will try again'. The bare third-person verb is meant as 'it' (the series). It can also be read as formal 'usted' ('You try again at 04:00:00') or as 'he/she tries'. Elsewhere the app uses 'tú', so the usted reading is unlikely but possible. More natural Spanish: 'Lo volverá a intentar' / 'Se volverá a intentar'. (d) '04:00:00' gives no date (today or tomorrow?) and no time zone, and seconds are odd in a time shown to users. (e) 'No se perdió nada' doesn't say what was not lost: funds, fees or progress. With a personal subject it could even mean 'it didn't miss anything' (perderse algo = to miss something), though context makes 'nothing was lost' clear. (f) The 'Por qué se detuvo' and 'Qué salió mal' lines have exactly the same text. That reads as redundant and may suggest two separate failures, or leave the reader wondering how 'why it stopped' differs from 'what went wrong'. (g) Two colons in one line (label: reason: explanation) make it hard to parse. The capital after the first colon is non-standard in Spanish prose but tolerable after a label. (h) 'pool' is an untranslated anglicism (common in crypto Spanish), and 'cambió' (changed) doesn't say how the pool changed.

2. Sesión privada 1: La red lo rechazó: el pool de Lovejoin cambió mientras se enviaba. No se perdió nada.
   - **Back:** Private session 1: The network rejected it: the Lovejoin pool changed while it was being sent. Nothing was lost.
   - **Notes:** (a) 'Sesión privada' is feminine but 'La red lo rechazó' uses masculine 'lo'. Grammatically, then, the network rejected something unnamed, not the session. In English, 'it' will naturally be read as the session. (b) The row sits in a list of things that could not be brought back, yet it ends 'No se perdió nada' (Nothing was lost). A reader may see a contradiction, or be unsure whether 'nada' means funds, the session or the attempt. (c) 'mientras se enviaba' has no subject. It could be the pool (carrying over the subject of 'cambió'), the session, or the unnamed 'lo'. English 'it' keeps the same uncertainty. (d) Nothing explains what a 'Sesión privada' is (a private mixing session?), and '1' is just a number. (e) The line has two colons in a row (label: reason: explanation), with a capital after the first.

3. La red lo rechazó: el pool de Lovejoin cambió mientras se enviaba. No se perdió nada. Volviendo a intentarlo en menos de un minuto.
   - **Back:** The network rejected it: the Lovejoin pool changed while it was being sent. Nothing was lost. Trying again in less than a minute.
   - **Notes:** (a) 'Volviendo a intentarlo en menos de un minuto' uses a lone gerund as a sentence, copying English UI style ('Retrying in...'). This is common in software but unnatural Spanish; 'Se volverá a intentar en menos de un minuto' would be more natural. The gerund suggests the retry is happening now, while 'en menos de un minuto' points ahead. So it can mean 'will retry within a minute' or 'is retrying, and that will take under a minute'. 'en' + a time can mean 'from now' or 'taking that long'; Peninsular usage prefers 'dentro de' for 'from now'. English 'Trying again in less than a minute' has the same two readings. (b) 'lo' could be the whole swap ('el swap' / 'el intercambio' is masculine, so it agrees) or a single submission within it. So it's unclear whether the whole swap was rejected or just one step. (c) 'mientras se enviaba' has no subject. Read with the subject of 'cambió', the pool is what was being sent; the intended subject is the rejected item. (d) 'No se perdió nada' doesn't say what was not lost (funds? progress?).

4. La última caja que no se fue: La red lo rechazó: el pool de Lovejoin cambió mientras se enviaba. No se perdió nada.
   - **Back:** The last box that didn't go: The network rejected it: the Lovejoin pool changed while it was being sent. Nothing was lost.
   - **Notes:** (a) 'La última caja que no se fue': 'última' can mean 'most recent' (the latest box that failed) or 'final' (the last box still left). 'que no se fue' uses 'irse' (to leave, go away), a vague, colloquial way to say a send failed. It could be read as 'didn't leave', 'didn't go out' or 'didn't go through'; I used 'didn't go' to keep it vague. The direction is also odd: the wallet is bringing boxes back, yet the label talks about a box that didn't leave. (b) 'caja' is feminine but 'La red lo rechazó' uses masculine 'lo', so grammatically the network rejected something other than the box. A Spanish reader would expect 'la rechazó' if the box were meant. In English, 'it' will be read as the box. (c) 'mientras se enviaba' has no subject: it could be the box, the pool (carrying over the subject of 'cambió'), or the unnamed 'lo'. (d) Two colons in one line, with a capital after the first, blur where the label ends and the message starts. (e) 'No se perdió nada' doesn't say what was not lost. (f) Minor: in a money app, 'caja' can also suggest cash, a till or a checkout, though context makes 'box' clear.

5. Se detuvo el regreso de las cajas a tu saldo privado: varios intentos seguidos no enviaron nada. La red lo rechazó: el pool de Lovejoin cambió mientras se enviaba. No se perdió nada. Pulsa “Volver a intentarlo” para traer de vuelta las que quedan.
   - **Back:** The return of the boxes to your private balance stopped: several attempts in a row didn't send anything. The network rejected it: the Lovejoin pool changed while it was being sent. Nothing was lost. Press “Try again” to bring back the ones that remain.
   - **Notes:** (a) 'Se detuvo' is preterite, kept as 'stopped'; for a current state, English would more naturally say 'has stopped'. The reflexive form leaves open whether it stopped by itself or the wallet stopped it. (b) 'varios intentos seguidos no enviaron nada' literally says the attempts sent nothing. That clashes with the next sentence, which says it was rejected while being sent, so something was sent. The intended sense is probably 'none of them got anything through'. (c) 'La red lo rechazó' uses singular masculine 'lo' right after the plural 'varios intentos' and the feminine plural 'las cajas'. The only matching noun in the text is 'el regreso' ('the network rejected the return'), which is probably not what's meant; the last attempt is more likely. English 'it' is just as unclear. (d) 'mientras se enviaba' has no subject: the pool (carrying over the subject of 'cambió') or the unnamed rejected item. (e) 'No se perdió nada' sits next to 'traer de vuelta las que quedan', so a reader may wonder what 'nada' covers when some boxes still haven't come back. (f) 'las que quedan' (the ones that remain) refers to 'las cajas', presumably the boxes not yet returned; it doesn't say how many. (g) 'Pulsa' leans Peninsular; Latin American Spanish more often uses 'Presiona', 'Toca' or 'Haz clic'. I rendered the button 'Volver a intentarlo' as 'Try again'; it must match the English button's actual label. (h) Two reasons are stacked after the colon (several attempts failed; the network rejected it), which reads as somewhat redundant.

### Japanese

1. 停止した理由: 送信中に Lovejoin のプールが変わったため、ネットワークが拒否しました。失われたものはありません。04:00:00 にもう一度試します。問題の内容: 送信中に Lovejoin のプールが変わったため、ネットワークが拒否しました。失われたものはありません。
   - **Back:** Reason it stopped: The network rejected it because the Lovejoin pool changed while it was being sent. Nothing has been lost. Will try again at 04:00:00. Details of the problem: The network rejected it because the Lovejoin pool changed while it was being sent. Nothing has been lost.
   - **Notes:** - The Japanese never says what the network rejected (拒否しました) or what was being sent (送信中). English needs an object, so I used "it". It could mean one transaction in the series, the whole series, or something else. The subject of 停止した (stopped) and of 試します (will try) is also left out. The context suggests the series and the wallet.
- 送信 is also the normal word for the wallet's Send feature. So 送信中に can be read as "while you were sending" something, not only "while the transaction was being submitted". It doesn't say who was sending. 送信中に most naturally goes with 変わった (the pool changed during sending). It could also be read as applying to 拒否しました.
- プールが変わった: "changed" could mean the pool's contents changed (for example, boxes in it were used) or that it became a different pool. Nothing says who or what changed it, so a user might think they caused it.
- 失われたものはありません: "nothing" (もの, things) doesn't say what: funds, boxes, fees, or progress.
- 04:00:00 に means "at 04:00:00", a clock time. It gives no date (today or tomorrow?) and no time zone, and if the app uses a 12-hour clock it could be AM or PM. The leading zero and the seconds make it look like a countdown ("in 4 hours"). If a delay was meant, it should be 後に. A clock time with seconds is unusual in Japanese prose.
- もう一度 literally means "one more time". That suggests a single extra attempt, not ongoing retries.
- 停止した (stopped) next to a scheduled retry leaves it unclear whether the series has stopped for good or is only paused.
- Because the text runs together, it isn't clear whether the retry sentence belongs to the "Reason it stopped" line or is a separate line.
- The same reason appears word for word under "Reason it stopped" and "Details of the problem". The two labels mean nearly the same thing, so the second line adds nothing and looks like a duplicate.

2. プライベートセッション 1: 送信中に Lovejoin のプールが変わったため、ネットワークが拒否しました。失われたものはありません。
   - **Back:** Private session 1: The network rejected it because the Lovejoin pool changed while it was being sent. Nothing has been lost.
   - **Notes:** - "1" could be a position in the list or a session number or ID. The text doesn't say how a "private session" relates to boxes.
- The row only gives a reason. It doesn't say the session could not be brought back; it relies on the list heading for that.
- As in item 1, it doesn't say what was rejected or what was being sent ("it"). Here a reader may take "it" to be the private session itself.
- "Nothing has been lost" in a list of things that could not be brought back can read as a contradiction. It could mean the session's funds are safe but not yet returned, or that the failed attempt cost nothing. It doesn't say what was not lost.
- Unlike items 1 and 3, there is no retry time or next step. It is unclear whether the wallet will retry or the user must act.
- 送信中 and プールが変わった have the same two readings as in item 1 (who was sending; contents changed or a different pool).

3. 送信中に Lovejoin のプールが変わったため、ネットワークが拒否しました。失われたものはありません。1 分以内にもう一度試します。
   - **Back:** The network rejected it because the Lovejoin pool changed while it was being sent. Nothing has been lost. Will try again within 1 minute.
   - **Notes:** - It doesn't say what was rejected ("it"). The swap isn't mentioned, so it could be the swap's transaction or one step of it.
- Who will try again isn't said. The ます form reads as the wallet's own action, not an instruction to the user.
- もう一度 ("one more time") suggests a single extra attempt. It doesn't say what happens if that also fails. 1 分以内に ("within 1 minute") is clear.
- 送信中 doesn't say who was sending. Because the wallet is finishing the swap by itself, a reader may wonder whether "sending" means something they did.
- "Nothing has been lost" doesn't say what: the funds in the swap, or fees.
- プールが変わった has the same two readings as in item 1.

4. 最後に戻らなかったボックスの理由: 送信中に Lovejoin のプールが変わったため、ネットワークが拒否しました。失われたものはありません。
   - **Back:** Reason for the last box that did not come back: The network rejected it because the Lovejoin pool changed while it was being sent. Nothing has been lost.
   - **Notes:** - The main ambiguity is 最後に戻らなかったボックスの理由. 最後に can mean:
  - "most recently": the latest box that failed to come back
  - "final": the last box in the sequence, which did not come back
  - "in the end": the box that ultimately did not come back
  - "last time": the box or boxes that did not come back in the last run
  Japanese doesn't mark number, so "box" could also be "boxes". The English "last" keeps some of this ambiguity.
- ボックスの理由 ("the box's reason") is an unnatural phrase. A reason belongs to the failure, not the box, so "the reason it did not come back" has to be inferred.
- 戻らなかった is intransitive ("did not come back"), as if the box returns by itself. The transitive 戻せなかった would mean "could not be brought back". The past tense suggests the attempt for that box is over, but it doesn't say whether the box will be retried or skipped while the others continue.
- It doesn't say what was rejected or being sent ("it"). Here it naturally reads as the box.
- "Nothing has been lost" right after a box that did not come back can read as a contradiction (lost or just not yet returned?).
- The verb is 戻る here, but item 5 uses 引き戻し and 戻す for what seems to be the same action.

5. すべてのボックスの引き戻しが止まりました。続けて数回試しましたが、何も送信できませんでした。送信中に Lovejoin のプールが変わったため、ネットワークが拒否しました。失われたものはありません。残りのボックスを戻すには「もう一度試す」を押してください。
   - **Back:** Pulling back all the boxes has stopped. Tried several times in a row, but could not send anything. The network rejected it because the Lovejoin pool changed while it was being sent. Nothing has been lost. To bring back the remaining boxes, press "Try again".
   - **Notes:** - すべてのボックスの引き戻しが止まりました has two readings depending on what すべて (all) applies to:
  - The operation: the "bring back all boxes" process has stopped, partway through.
  - The boxes: pulling back has stopped for every box, which suggests none came back.
  The later 残りのボックス ("remaining boxes") implies some boxes already came back, which conflicts with the second reading.
- 引き戻し ("pulling back") is an uncommon noun. It differs from 戻す ("bring back") in the last sentence and 戻る in item 4, so a reader may think these are different actions (for example a withdrawal versus a reversal).
- 止まりました (intransitive "stopped") doesn't say whether the wallet stopped on purpose after the failures or it simply stopped. The subject of 試しました (tried) is also not stated. 数回 ("several/a few times") is vague.
- 何も送信できませんでした ("could not send anything") conflicts with the next sentence. If the network rejected it, something was sent. So "send" must mean "get through / be accepted", not "transmit".
- Only one reason is given. It doesn't say whether it applies to every attempt or only the last one, and "it" (what was rejected) is not stated.
- "Nothing has been lost" doesn't say what was not lost.
- 「もう一度試す」 literally means "try once more" and matches the button name. 押してください is a plain polite request (press, tap or click).
