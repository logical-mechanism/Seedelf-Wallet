## Notes

This was a blind check: I opened no file, saw none of the English originals and used no tool except StructuredOutput.

Pairs that can read as contradicting each other:
1. es 9 vs es 5-7. 'Lo que tiene cada uno no está en la transacción' uses a free relative ('what each one has'), which names the funds themselves rather than information about them. A native reader can take it as 'their contents aren't part of the transaction', which clashes with 'Gasta N UTxO(s) de contrato que contiene(n) X ₳' on the same screen. ja 17 avoids this by using an embedded question (何を保持しているか, 'what each one holds' as information). An embedded question would fix the Spanish too, e.g. 'La transacción no dice qué tiene cada uno'.
2. Switches vs row labels. es 2 and ja 11 acknowledge only that the amount leaves the contract. Neither repeats the 'not toward you / does not reach you' half of es 1 and ja 10, though that is the part the warning is about. So turning the switch on never states that the user knows the amount won't come to them.
3. es 2 vs ja 11 commit to different things. 'Sé que…' is awareness ('I know'), while '想定どおりです' is expectation or intent ('as expected'). ja 11 can also be read as the wallet reassuring the user ('this is expected/normal'), which undercuts the warning. Japanese acknowledgement switches usually say '…ことを理解しました' or '…ことを確認しました'. es 2 has a second reading as well: 'salir de' also means 'come from', so it can be read as 'I know this [request] comes from the contract'.
4. es 1 vs the notes. es 1 describes 'it' with no word for 'portion'. If the row's amount differs from the note's X ₳ (what the contract UTxOs hold), a Spanish reader may expect the two to match and be confused. ja 10's '分' ('the portion that…') explains why they can differ.
5. I found no contradiction between es 1 'no hacia ti' and es 3/4 'no vuelve a ti'. 'vuelve' presupposes the funds were yours, which es 3 ('si alguno es tuyo') and es 4 ('tu propia orden') supply. The same holds for ja 10 '届かない' vs ja 12/13 '戻ってきません'. es 3 vs es 4 ('ayuda a liberar' vs 'libera') is also consistent.

Unnatural or weak wording:
6. ja 15 '保持額は X ₳ とトークンです' doesn't fit: 額 is a sum of money, and 'the held amount is X ₳ and tokens' mixes a sum with a kind of asset. '保持額' itself is uncommon (保有額 is the usual word). In ja 14 and 15 it also stands in its own sentence with no owner, so it's unclear whose total it is: these UTxOs, each one, the contract, or the user. Something like '…X ₳ とトークンを保持しています' or a parenthetical '（合計 X ₳）' reads more naturally.
7. ja 14 and 15 say '使用します' ('uses'), which is weaker than es 'Gasta' ('spends') and could cover merely referencing a UTxO.
8. ja 13 '注文または出品がコントラクトから解放されます' reads as translationese: 解放 (release) goes with funds or locks, not orders or listings. ja 12 and 13 each repeat あなた three times, which also sounds translated.
9. es 4 has three issues:
   - 'liberar una orden' also has a business/ERP sense, 'release an order for execution (approve it)'. Read that way it says 'your signature lets your own order go ahead', which takes the edge off the warning.
   - 'de venta' can cover both nouns or only 'publicación'.
   - 'publicación de venta' is understandable, but 'anuncio de venta' is more usual.
10. es 3: 'Si alguno es tuyo' counts 'fondos' as separate items, which is slightly odd. 'Si parte es tuya' or 'Si alguno de ellos es tuyo' is smoother. In both es 3 and ja 12, the first sentence ('ayuda a liberar' / '助けになります') sounds like a benefit, and the warning only arrives in the second sentence.
11. es 5 (singular): in 'UTxO de contrato que contiene X ₳', the relative clause can attach to 'contrato' (the contract holds X ₳). The plural forms avoid this, but there 'que contienen X ₳' can mean each UTxO or all of them together.
12. es 9: 'su fuente de datos' can be read as the website's data source, since this hint appears when a site asked for the signature. 'cada uno' can also mean 'each person'.
13. es 8 and ja 16 never say the hash beside them is the user's own key. They can read as a call to action ('sign now') rather than a description.
14. Minor style points:
   - es 'UTxOs' adds a plural -s, against the RAE's advice that acronyms stay invariable ('los UTxO'), though the -s form is common in crypto Spanish.
   - ja '{{count}} 件' puts a space before the counter, a house-style choice that's a little unusual outside tech docs.
15. This batch had no es 'contractInputsTokens_one'. If the tokens note can appear with a count of 1, that form wasn't checked here.
