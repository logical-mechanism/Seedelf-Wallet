### Round one's notes, es

I did this check blind: no file opened, and StructuredOutput was the only tool used.

WHAT A NATIVE READER WOULD FIND UNNATURAL
1. contractAck: 'Tengo previsto que...' first reads as 'I have planned that...'. It is an odd fit for an acknowledgement switch, because the user seems to declare an intention rather than show they understand. 'Entiendo que esto no volverá a mí' or 'Acepto que esto no vuelva a mí' would be more idiomatic. 'Esto' (this) never says what 'this' is.
2. contractRow: 'no hacia ti' (not towards you) is stiff for money. 'no para ti' or 'no te llega' sounds more natural.
3. contractYours:
   - 'y no vuelven a ti' after 'Puede que sean tuyos' wants 'pero' (but) or 'y aun así' (and even so).
   - 'tu clave' can read as 'your password' to a non-technical reader; 'tu clave pública' would remove that.
   - 'liberar fondos' in banking Spanish suggests funds being made available to their owner.
4. All nine 'Gasta {{count}} ...' strings start with a verb that has no subject, so they read like fragments. Because the wallet uses 'tú' everywhere else, 'Gasta' can also read as the imperative 'Spend!'. Spelling out the subject ('Esta transacción gasta...' or 'Se gasta(n)...') would remove both problems.
5. 'UTxOs' with an English plural -s is common in crypto Spanish but against the usual style-guide norm ('los UTxO'). This is minor.

PAIRS THAT CAN READ AS CONTRADICTING OR IN TENSION
(a) contractRow vs contractYours. If 'no hacia ti' is taken as 'not meant for you / not yours', it clashes with 'Puede que sean tuyos' (they may be yours). On the intended direction-only reading, the two agree.
(b) contractYours against itself: 'Tu firma libera fondos' (banking sense: unlocks funds for their owner) against 'no vuelven a ti'. The second sentence settles it, but the first impression points the wrong way.
(c) unknownInputsKeyReleases_* vs contractInputs*/contractInputsTokens*. Both families open with 'Gasta {{count}} UTxO(s)...' and no subject. Shown together, a reader may not see they count different UTxOs, so 'no se puede mostrar lo que contiene' (what it holds can't be shown) sits next to 'que contiene {{ada}} ₳' (contains X ₳). It is also unclear whether the counts overlap or add up.
(d) contractAck vs contractYours: no real contradiction. The switch puts the non-return as the user's expectation or plan (subjunctive 'no vuelva'), and the warning states it as fact (indicative 'no vuelven'). The meanings agree; only the mood differs.
(e) contractRow says 'un contrato' (one contract), while the notes count contract UTxOs that may come from several contracts. This is minor.

OTHER CHECKS
- Register is consistent: the user is 'tú' throughout (ti, tu, tuyos), and the switch speaks in the user's voice (mí).
- In the singular contractInputs_one and contractInputsTokens_one, 'que contiene' can attach to the contract instead of the UTxO, so the amount could read as the contract's whole holding. The plural forms avoid this.
- In the plurals, {{ada}} reads most naturally as a total, but 'each contains {{ada}}' is possible. 'que suman {{ada}} ₳' would make the total explicit.
- None of the unknownInputsKeyReleases forms says whose UTxOs these are, or that the user's key or signature releases them, although the key name suggests it. The text says only 'it spends'.
- Every _many form matches its _other form, which is correct for Spanish.

### Round one's notes, ja

This was a blind check: I used no tool other than StructuredOutput and opened no file.

POSSIBLE OMISSION. The key name unknownInputsKeyReleases suggests the English says the user's key unlocks or releases these UTxOs. All three Japanese forms say only "(this) uses N UTxOs the wallet couldn't find, so their contents can't be shown". They mention no key, no signature, no release and no risk. If the English has that clause, it is missing from the Japanese.

PAIRS THAT PULL AGAINST EACH OTHER (none is a flat contradiction):
- contractAck vs contractYours: the warning states as a fact that the funds will not come back to the user ("戻ってきません"). The switch has the user only "assume" it ("想定しています"), so it reads as "I expected this", not "I understand". That fits if the English is an expectation ("I expect..."). If the English is an acknowledgement ("I understand..."), 理解しています or 承知しています would match better.
- contractRow vs contractYours: the row's 分 ("the portion") implies only part of the contract's funds misses the user, while the warning says flatly that they won't come back. If the row's figure is smaller than the total in contractInputs, a reader may take the warning to cover the whole total.
- Two different verbs for the same money: contractRow says 届かない ("doesn't reach you"), while contractAck and contractYours say 戻ってこない / 戻ってきません ("doesn't come back"). "Come back" presupposes the funds were the user's before; "reach" doesn't.
- Two different verbs for spending an input: unknownInputsKeyReleases says 使用する ("uses"), contractInputs and contractInputsTokens say 消費します ("consumes"). "Use" is weaker and could read as the transaction merely referencing a UTxO. "Contents" is also worded two ways: 内容 in one, 中身 in the other.
- If unknownInputsKeyReleases and contractInputs appear together, one says "can't show their contents" and the other "their contents are X ₳". They cover different UTxOs, but the Japanese never says the unfound UTxOs are separate from the contract ones, so a reader may wonder whether X ₳ includes them.

UNNATURAL TO A NATIVE READER:
- contractYours uses あなた three times in two sentences, which reads like a translation; Japanese would drop most of them. 鍵が記載された is document vocabulary and can suggest the secret key itself is written in the contract. あなたの鍵が指定されたコントラクト ("a contract in which your key is specified") keeps the meaning and reads more naturally. 解放されます can briefly read as good news.
- contractRow is long and sentence-like for a row label, with a comma in the middle. It is understandable, and 届かない分 can carry a "money owed to you that you won't get" sense.
- contractAck's 想定しています is business register. It is natural enough, but a little stiff for a personal switch, and これ only gets its meaning from context.
- contractInputs and contractInputsTokens: with a count above 1, it is open whether {{ada}} is the total or per UTxO. 合計 {{ada}} ₳ would settle it. 中身 is a little casual for a wallet.

PLURAL FORMS. Japanese has no plural, so identical _one, _other and _many texts are correct. Each reads naturally for any count, and 件 is a fine counter for UTxOs.

## Round two: dappUi.warn.contractYours, reworded

The English was now "Your signature releases funds from a contract that names you. They may be yours, but they don't come back to you." Each agent saw only its own language's value, plus the row label and the switch beside it.

**es:** "Tu firma libera fondos de un contrato que te menciona. Puede que sean tuyos, pero no vuelven a ti."
- **Back-translation:** "Your signature releases funds from a contract that mentions you. It may be that they are yours, but they do not come back to you."
- **The negation:** it sits outside the "puede que" (subjunctive "sean", indicative "vuelven"). Only the ownership is hedged.
- **No reading of a secret exposed:** "mencionar names or refers; it never means revealing a secret."
- **Notes:**
  - "te menciona" is vague and sounds translated, and "un contrato en el que figuras" is more idiomatic;
  - "libera fondos" leans to good news until the second sentence, as "releases" does.

**ja:** "署名すると、あなたを指定したコントラクトから資金が解放されます。あなたのものかもしれませんが、戻ってきません。"
- **Back-translation:** "If you sign, funds will be released from the contract that designated you. They may be yours, but they will not come back."
- **No reading of a secret exposed.**
- **The negation:** definite and total.
- **Notes:**
  - あなたを指定した leaves open "designated you as what?";
  - the past tense 指定した is slightly off for a standing state;
  - "not to you" could be said outright;
  - 解放 leans to good news.

## Round three: the lead's answer to round two

**es:** "Tu firma libera fondos de un contrato en el que figuras. Puede que sean tuyos, pero no vuelven a ti."
- **Back-translation:** "Your signature releases funds from a contract in which you appear. It may be that they are yours, but they do not return to you."
- **The negation:** outside the "maybe".
- **References:** "tuyos" can only be the funds, and "en el que" the contract.
- **No reading of a password, PIN, key or phrase exposed.**
- **Consistent** with "Desde un contrato, no hacia ti" and "Tengo previsto que esto no vuelva a mí".
- **Note:** "volver" presumes the funds were the user's, which "puede que sean tuyos" leaves open; it is the word that ties the warning to the switch, so keep it.

**ja:** "署名すると、あなたが記載されているコントラクトから資金が解放されます。あなたのものかもしれませんが、手元には戻りません。"
- **Back-translation:** "When you sign, funds are released from the contract in which you are listed. They may be yours, but they will not come back to your hands."
- **The negation:** flat and unhedged, with no "not all" reading.
- **No reading of a secret exposed.**
- **Consistent** with the row and the switch: 戻らない and 戻ってこない, あなた becoming 自分.
- **Note:** 記載 is paper-document vocabulary, a slight mix of registers beside コントラクト.

Both values were kept as round three read them.
