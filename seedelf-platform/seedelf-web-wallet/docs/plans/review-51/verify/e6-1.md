From lane E6, graded medium by its finder.

## Trace: confirmed, medium

Every step goes through in TREE (minted-by.ts, balances.ts, RemoveSeedelf.tsx, activity.ts, withdraw.ts and en.json are unchanged since web-wallet/1.9.1). The mint policy at 5b82530 is open and checks no output. Every Seedelf's register can be read through its name. So a stranger can mint their own Seedelf G under a fresh re-randomization of the user's register and pay the user's public account 1 ₳ in the same transaction T.

What the wallet does with it:
- Both contract-scan paths count G's UTxO as owned (g^x == u). seedelfSide lists G and asks paidByOf.
- There's no record of who paid. Two pieces of evidence a stranger can plant both answer "account": T's output at the account, which is in `held.account` (credential_utxos by the account's payment keys), and T's Public activity entry, which is in `held.accountTxs` (every entry, a `received` one with no fee included).
- So RemoveSeedelf starts on "Public account" with Review enabled. The card says "Links nothing new: it paid for this Seedelf". The note says "Back where this Seedelf's ADA came from: your public account paid for it, so this links nothing new."
- The removal pays the active account's 0/0 (withdraw.ts:179). WebAssembly checks only that the register is owned (lib.rs:5019-5023).
- Without the 1 ₳ output, paidByOf returns undefined: nothing is chosen, and the note recommends the private balance.

My test runs this with the real services and screen, and fails on TREE because of the bug. With the finder's fix sketch in a copy (since removed), it passes, and only the two old tests that pin the inference (minted-by.test.ts:55, :129) fail there.

When it costs privacy: the claim "your public account paid for it" is always false for a planted Seedelf. But a new link appears only when the user's Seedelf name X isn't already tied to the account the 1 ₳ hit. That covers three cases:
- X paid for from the private balance out of money received (privacy.md:107: the case that option is for);
- X paid for by another of the user's accounts: paidByAccount becomes the active account, so otherAccountPaid can't fire, and the stranger turns off privacy.md:191's safeguard and ties two accounts together;
- X made by another tool.

For a Seedelf paid for by the same account (the default flow), the stranger learns nothing new. The finder's own setup ("suspects A is theirs") assumes this, so the finding stands as described.

Grade: medium. It's "a privacy link made by default". A stranger sets it up, and it needs one ordinary user action: Remove is the only way to get back the ADA locked with the Seedelf, and the stranger picks that amount. No money is lost, and the review truthfully says the ADA comes back to the public account, so it isn't high.

Not known: nothing in the backlog, the archive, privacy.md or SECURITY.md covers a stranger steering the who-paid inference. It isn't social engineering (no contact: the wallet's own default and words do it), and it isn't a dishonest source (a stranger's rows inside an honest answer, which the brief puts in scope).

## Impact: partly, low

The mechanism is real. A stranger can plant the who-paid evidence, and Remove then preselects the public account and says the removal links nothing new. The harm is much narrower than the scenario claims, though.

1. **The default case links nothing new.** The wallet's default order is to create the first Seedelf from the public account (privacy.md:80, :106 "This is the default"). That mint already ties X to the account that paid for it, in the open. Removing G to that same account adds nothing, and the attacker could have read the account off X's own mint without planting anything. The scenario's result, "the attacker has now tied Seedelf X to public account A", is new only where X isn't already tied to the active account:
   - (a) X was created from the private balance out of money received (privacy.md:107, the README's escape from its first implicit tracking method), so the attack ties X to the account;
   - (b) another of the user's accounts paid for X. The attack then ties the two accounts and gets around chunk 18's "chooses nothing ... and warns" (privacy.md:191).
2. **It also needs all of these:**
   - a correct guess at an address of the victim's *active* account (only that account's UTxOs and Activity count);
   - the user opening Manage, then Remove, on a Seedelf they never created;
   - the user keeping the preselected card through Review.

   Each probe costs about 3 ₳, most of which goes to the victim if the guess is right.
3. **What the user loses:** no money. They get about 1.3 ₳ back. The link is seen through the attacker's own `d`, which they can publish to make it provable.
4. **Ways out:** pick the private-balance card, or leave G alone. The private-side twin costs no more than a dust payment, and the unknown-payer advice points to private anyway.

This is a targeted confirmation attack. It works only on non-default setups and needs the user's cooperation, so it's an edge case. I was unsure between low and medium, so I took the lower, as the brief says. Two precedents could support medium: privacy review §3.2 graded the always-public default High, and independent review M12 graded a false privacy assurance Medium. Both reached every user. This doesn't, and that's why low is the fairer grade by reach.

**Fix notes:**

The finder's direction holds, with one correction to the evidence it keys on.

- **What counts as "the account paid".** The account must have spent an input of the mint. In Activity that's an entry carrying `fee`: activity.ts:904 sets it only when `spent`, and spending needs the account's signature.
  - Don't key on the entry's kind. It's a derived classification, not a signed fact.
  - Never key on an account UTxO from the mint, or on a `received` entry. Both only show the transaction paid the account.
  - So balances.ts:241-242 must pass `held()` the entries (txHash plus fee), not bare hashes. Drop minted-by.ts:124's `held.account` test.
- **Otherwise return undefined**, so nothing is chosen, as §3.2 and chunk 18 decided.
