# Seedelf Wallet privacy policy

**Effective 23 November 2026.** What changed, and when, is under *Changes* at the end. This policy covers the Seedelf Wallet browser extension, published by Logical Mechanism LLC.

**In short:** no accounts, analytics, crash reports or ads. Two services the wallet uses are ours. On mainnet, it reads Cardano and sends your transactions through our server, `mainnet.seedelf.logicalmechanism.io`, which sees your IP address, your public account and each transaction you send, and logs neither your IP address nor what you ask. giveme.my receives your IP address and each private transaction you send, to add its collateral. Neither ever gets your recovery phrase or your keys. To work, the extension also talks to Koios, a public Cardano API: on preprod for everything, and on mainnet for anything our server can't answer, or for everything if you turn our server off in Settings (messages still go through ours). On mainnet it also talks to CoinGecko, for ADA's price. When you swap, it also talks to Minswap, and when you ask to see an NFT's image, to Blockfrost's IPFS gateway. Nothing else: Lovejoin, the mixer, is a contract on the Cardano blockchain, reached through those servers. Sites see your public account, or a private session's account, only if you turn on connecting sites and connect them. Messages are off until you turn them on: then the wallet reads and posts sealed messages through our server, which sees your IP address and when, never what a message says, and can't tell from a message whom it's for.

The extension runs on Cardano mainnet, and can switch to preprod, Cardano's test network, on the welcome screen or in Settings. Everything below applies to both; each network has its own services' addresses, listed below. **The Cardano server**, below, is the one the wallet reads the blockchain and sends transactions through: on mainnet ours, and Koios for anything ours can't answer, or for everything if you turn ours off; on preprod, Koios.

Signing a site's transaction that spends someone else's UTxOs asks **the Cardano server** about those UTxOs. |

## The services the extension talks to

Every request goes without your browser's cookies or a referrer, and none is kept in your browser's cache. Chrome remembers for up to two hours, in memory, that our Cardano server accepts the wallet's requests that send it something (a check browsers make before such a request), so later ones skip that step. It keeps each such request's address, never what it sends, and it's gone when the browser closes.

- **The Cardano server**, which the wallet reads the blockchain and sends your transactions through:
  - **On mainnet, ours:** `mainnet.seedelf.logicalmechanism.io`, **run by Logical Mechanism**, who publish this extension, on a DigitalOcean server in New York. It reads a Cardano node, db-sync and Kupo that Logical Mechanism runs elsewhere, which never see you: only our server reaches them. A transaction you send reaches the network from Logical Mechanism's stake pool relay, whose address its registration already publishes.
    - Neither its web server nor its API logs your IP address or what you ask. It holds your IP address in memory only, to limit how often one address can ask, and keeps no record of it. DigitalOcean, which hosts it, sees connections to it, not what's in them.
    - Its name is seen on your network: your internet provider, your Wi-Fi's owner or your DNS service can tell from it that this device uses Seedelf Wallet on mainnet, and when, though not what it asks.
    - Whatever part it can't answer, the wallet asks Koios instead, for five minutes, then ours again. **Read Cardano through Koios only**, in Settings → Network, turns ours off for reading Cardano and sending transactions; messages, while Messages is on, still go through ours (below).
  - **Koios** (`api.koios.rest` on mainnet, `preprod.koios.rest` on preprod), a public Cardano API run by the Koios community: everything on preprod, and on mainnet whatever ours can't answer, or everything with that switch on. Even while ours answers, the wallet asks Koios what made your boxes in a later pool of the same box value as one shown, which ours doesn't cover (below).
  - Whichever answers sees your IP address and what the wallet asks: your public account and its staking, one account at a time (though while a connected site uses a different public account from the one you're looking at, it sees both, from one IP address), the whole Seedelf contract (from ours, what changed since your last reading, which tells it roughly when that was), your open private sessions' one-time accounts, all in one request, when you open or refresh the dApps page or the Swaps screen, so it can tie them to each other, Lovejoin's pools when you use Lovejoin, and at times what made some of your Lovejoin boxes (below), the pools and DReps you look at, your own DRep and its votes, each NFT whose image you ask to see, and the transactions you submit, with a check of each one's status.
  - After a restore, or when you ask, the wallet looks for your recovery phrase's other public accounts: one request for each account, never one for all of them, but from one IP address seconds apart, so the Cardano server can still guess they're one wallet's.
  - After a restore, on the network you restore on, or when you press **Find leftovers** on the dApps page, it also looks for what your private sessions' one-time accounts still hold: it asks the Cardano server about those accounts 20 at a time, those your next private sessions will use among them, then about the used ones' money, transactions and orders, and the first Seedelf UTxO each later payment into them spent, all from one IP address, so the server can tell they're one wallet's, the sessions you open next too, once they're funded. The sessions it finds join your list of swaps, and whether it has finished looking on each network is kept with that list, on your device only, encrypted like your contacts.
  - From what the wallet reads, it can tell that your Cardano account uses Seedelf, and guess that you use Lovejoin, but not which Seedelf UTxOs are yours: that check runs on your device. Nor, from reading the pool, which Lovejoin boxes are yours. But when the wallet has no record of how one of your boxes went into the pool (after a restore, a box someone else's mix has moved, or one someone else deposited under your Seedelf's key), and Koios is answering, it asks Koios about the transaction that made it before **Mix my boxes again** takes it, before it comes back by itself from one of Lovejoin's current pools, or when you press **Bring one back now** or **Bring one back** (about those it might take ahead of one it knows, together), each again the next time where Koios didn't answer, and at a pool read that finds more of your boxes than it expects (after a restore, say), about all such boxes together, and again at each later pool read that counts your boxes, **Bring one back anyway**'s included, about one of those it hasn't answered for yet, until it does, and, while it waits in one of Lovejoin's current pools with no time set to come back, about that pool's other boxes of yours the wallet has no record or answer for, together. That tells Koios which boxes are yours, from your IP address. Ours answers that for every box in every pool it shows alike, so it learns nothing from it; for a box in a later pool of the same box value as one shown, which ours doesn't cover, Koios is asked even while ours answers. While a transaction of yours that touches your private balance or Lovejoin is on its way, ours is asked every five seconds what changed, so it can tell you're waiting for one, not which. But every transaction goes to the Cardano server to be sent, from the same IP address that reads your public account. So it can see which private UTxOs and Lovejoin boxes you spend, and the new ones they pay into, and tie them to your public account. That includes both ends of Lovejoin.
  - Two reviews go to it before you press Send: creating a Seedelf paid by your public account, whose draft shows it the Seedelf's tag, and a Lovejoin mix or return, whose first mix in each pool it measures, which tells it the pools a return would use. Once a private session's return is sent, it also measures the one mix of the session's 5 ₳ collateral, where there is one, before giveme.my witnesses it.
  - **Lovejoin's pools.** Anyone can start one, so the wallet looks for new ones: on mainnet it asks ours for its list of pools, the same for everyone, as it reads the pools, and again only after five minutes while the browser keeps the wallet running; on preprod, with Koios only, or when ours hasn't answered, it reads from **Koios** what's new at Lovejoin's reference holder, the transactions that started those pools and their scripts' registrations, when you open Lovejoin's page or a pool's start. It also looks, by our server's list or by Koios as above, at most every five minutes while the browser keeps the wallet running, when you press **Bring every box back now** in Settings, and, while it knows of no pool of 5 ₳ boxes on Lovejoin's current contracts, as a private session's return is built, by you or by the wallet as a swap that runs itself comes back, at a swap's review or its Stop, at a session's funding or top-up review, at the review of a mix from your private balance and as the wallet builds that mix, and when you put 5 ₳ in for Lovejoin, at the moment of each. Where it finds a later pool of the same box value as one it shows, it reads that pool's boxes once, so a restore finds any of yours there. None of that names anything of yours, but one thing: if some of your boxes are in such a later pool, the wallet reads it at every pool read until they've all left it, which tells the Cardano server you hold boxes there. And a pool this device read before an earlier one of the same box value turned up stays among the pools it reads, so no box of yours there drops out of view: such a device reads one more pool at each pool read than one that never saw it, which tells the Cardano server only that this device saw that pool first. Before the wallet builds a Lovejoin mix or a box's way back, at a review too, it asks what sits in that pool's reward account, the one Lovejoin's own script runs through, and again as you send a mix or a return kept for you: one request a pool, the same for everyone, naming the pool and nothing of yours. Starting a pool asks the Cardano server, only as you press **Start the pool**, whether the pool's script is registered already: that names the pool you're starting, seconds before the blockchain shows it to everyone.
  - The wallet asks the network it's on. It asks the other only to finish what you started there: a payment on its way, a swap, or a Lovejoin mix or box. Messages is the one exception (below): its server is asked for every network whose Messages is on, whichever network the wallet shows.
- **Messages** (Seedelf Messaging), only on a network whose **Messages** switch you turn on (they're off until you do): our server, `mainnet.seedelf.logicalmechanism.io`, for both networks' messages, **run by Logical Mechanism**. The wallet reads and posts for every network whose switch is on, whichever network it shows, and **Read Cardano through Koios only** doesn't change that.
  - Each message is sealed on your device to its reader's Seedelf key. The server keeps it 36 hours, in memory and in a file on its disk so a restart keeps it, and every wallet with Messages on downloads every message, so it can't read one or tell from it whom it's for, and nothing it holds is a key anyone could pay to see who spends it. Nor does it keep any account, sender or recipient. Anyone can download the messages and keep a copy past the 36 hours: whoever later gets your recovery phrase could read, from that copy, the first messages written to you and what their writers sent before your first reply.
  - It sees your IP address each time the wallet reads the messages, while the wallet is unlocked: at unlock, every minute after, and every 10 seconds while a page of the wallet is on screen. So it can tell when a device at your IP address is unlocked with Messages on, and from the 10 seconds, when the wallet is open on screen. Each read starts where the wallet's last one ended, so it could tell a wallet that comes back from another IP address is the same one. And over many reads, timing them could let it test whether messages it sealed itself are yours: the wallet's next read can come a few milliseconds late while it takes in its own.
  - It sees each message you post, with your IP address, when, and its size (padded to 512 bytes, 2, 8 or 32 KiB). To stop floods it counts each address's posts (an IPv6 address by its /56), in memory only. It counts recent posts to ask for more proof of work as they grow: that count halves every 10 minutes and is forgotten once it's small. It also counts the messages and bytes each address posted in each hour, to limit what one address can have on the server: each hour's count is kept 36 hours after the hour ends, then forgotten. And it counts the bytes of messages it sent each address in each hour, to limit what one address reads in a day: each hour's count is kept a day after the hour ends, then forgotten. The counts say how many, never which messages, and are never logged or written to disk. Every page of messages it sends says what a post from the reader's address would cost, which rises with that address's recent posts, so anyone sharing your address (a household, a VPN's exit) can watch roughly how much it posts. Neither its web server nor its API logs your IP address or what you ask, as above.
  - Writing first to a Seedelf, and showing one of yours (**Show my Seedelf**), each read the Seedelf contract on the Cardano server as you press, as Send does: the same read whichever Seedelf, so that server doesn't learn which, even with Messages off. The person you write to sees an unknown sender, unless what you write, or a Seedelf you show them, says who you are. But your first message gives them a randomized form of your Seedelf key, which anyone can pay: a payment under it, or under it added to one of your Seedelfs', that your wallet later spends, or a Lovejoin box it brings back, can tell them on the blockchain whether you hold that Seedelf. Their wallet checks a Seedelf you show only when they press its check, by a read of the whole contract.
  - Its name is seen on your network as above, on preprod too while preprod's Messages are on.
- **giveme.my** (`www.giveme.my`, at `/mainnet/collateral/` or `/preprod/collateral/`), a collateral service for Seedelf and Lovejoin transactions, **run by Logical Mechanism**, who publish this extension.
  - It sees your IP address and each transaction it adds collateral to, before it's on the blockchain: each private payment, each private session's funding, each Lovejoin box you bring back (with the private UTxO that pays for it, when a pool's boxes can't pay their own way back), each Lovejoin pool you start from your private balance, and the one mix of a private session's 5 ₳ collateral in Lovejoin, which names the session's one-time account. A box brought back with your public account paying instead uses your account's own collateral, and so names your account. From one IP address it can group them as one person's, including a session's funding with its collateral's mix and its Lovejoin boxes coming back.
  - Shared collateral keeps your own address out of those transactions.
  - Its own logs leave out raw IP addresses, and the transaction's hash whenever it adds collateral. The platform it runs on may keep its own request logs.
- **Minswap** (`agg-api.minswap.org` on mainnet, `aggr.monorepo-testnet-preprod.minswap.org` on preprod), only when you swap. Its aggregator quotes the swap and builds it for the swap's one-time account. The wallet doesn't ask it for that account's orders, or to cancel one: it reads them from the Cardano server and cancels them itself.
  - It sees your IP address, a token you search for that isn't on the wallet's own list, and each swap: its tokens and amounts and the one-time account's address.
  - It isn't told your private balance or your public account, but your IP address lets it group your swaps, and tie them to anything else done at Minswap from that address.
- **CoinGecko** (`api.coingecko.com`), on mainnet only, for ADA's price in the currency you chose (US dollars unless you change it).
  - It sees your IP address, and a request for ADA's price when Home or the swap form opens, at most every five minutes. It's told nothing about your wallet.
  - Choosing no currency in Settings stops it. On preprod it's never contacted: test ADA has no price.
- **Blockfrost's IPFS gateway** (`ipfs.blockfrost.dev`, on both networks), only when you press **Show image** on an NFT whose image is on IPFS. The first time, Chrome asks you to let the wallet reach it, unless the access you gave for connecting sites already covers it. A governance action's full text on IPFS opens there too, as a link (below).
  - It sees your IP address and which image file you ask for, which names that NFT. It's told nothing else about your wallet.
  - Nothing is fetched from it until you press the button, and the wallet keeps nothing it sends on your device past locking.
- **Cardanoscan** (`cardanoscan.io` on mainnet, `preprod.cardanoscan.io` on preprod), a blockchain explorer, only when you click a link to a transaction or an address. It opens in your browser, which then sends it your IP address and its own cookies, and keeps the page in your browser history. On a private transaction, the link says so.
- **A governance action's full text**, only when you click **Read its full text** on one whose text is on IPFS. Each service's own policy covers what it receives. Our Cardano server and giveme.my are ours; Koios, Minswap, CoinGecko, Blockfrost, Cardanoscan and GitHub aren't, and we don't control them. A VPN hides your IP address, not that your requests come from one wallet: the wallet reads your account and sends your private payments through the same connection.

If you use Chrome's Translate on the wallet, Chrome sends the page's text to Google, your balances, your addresses and the messages on screen among it, but not your recovery phrase: the wallet marks its words not to translate, so Translate neither sends them nor changes them. Otherwise the wallet doesn't turn Translate off: it speaks English, Spanish and Japanese, and using it in one of those avoids it.

**Lovejoin** isn't a service: it's a contract on the Cardano blockchain, and the wallet builds and signs its transactions itself, reading the chain through the Cardano server. Lovejoin's makers also run giveme.my, which sees each box you bring back with your IP address, and our Cardano server, which on mainnet sends each of Lovejoin's transactions the wallet makes. Lovejoin has had no third-party audit, only its makers' own review. Starting a pool is public: from your private balance, anyone can see that a spend from the Seedelf contract started it, and its change comes back into a new private UTxO; from your public account, that your account started it. About 23 ₳ of what it costs never comes back. Where a pool's boxes can't pay their own way back, bringing one back is paid from your private balance by default: one of its UTxOs goes into the box's transaction, and its ADA comes back with the box, less the fee, which ties that UTxO, and where it came from, to the box. Bringing it back with your public account paying instead ties the box, and the private UTxO it comes back into, to your account, and, for a box another of your public accounts put in, the two accounts to each other. Anyone can put a box into a pool under your Seedelf's key, read off its name, and then knows that box is yours, as a payer knows a payment. **Mix my boxes again** leaves out a box none of your accounts paid for; **Pay from my private balance anyway** takes it too, which ties that mix, its funding and its change to whoever put it in. One they then mix themselves reads as yours, though, so they can still follow it.

**Transactions are public.** Anything you submit is recorded on the Cardano blockchain, as with any wallet: amounts, tokens, timing, and which transactions spend which outputs. Seedelf hides who owns a UTxO from people reading the blockchain, and Lovejoin makes it harder for them to tell which box coming out was yours, not those. A box hides only among other people's boxes from the same pool that come back into a Seedelf around the same time, one of up to 9 at the default depth (16 with four boxes a mix) and fewer while few people use that pool, and not from the Cardano server or giveme.my. Spending boxes that came back together narrows it, and so does bringing one back soon after its mixes.

**Preprod and mainnet share your keys.** The same recovery phrase has the same keys on both networks, so anyone comparing the two blockchains can tell your preprod account is your mainnet one. Test on preprod with a phrase you don't use on mainnet if that matters to you.

## What we don't do

- We don't sell your data or share it for advertising. The wallet sends the services above what this page lists, for the reasons it gives, and every transaction you send is public on the Cardano blockchain. Our own two services receive only what's above: our Cardano server, to answer the wallet, pass its transactions on and, with Messages on, carry its sealed messages, and giveme.my, to add its collateral. Each one's entry above says what it keeps.
- We don't track what you do in the extension or on other websites.
- The extension doesn't read web pages. Only if you turn on connecting sites does it add the standard Cardano wallet entry (`window.cardano.seedelf`) to https pages, and to pages served from this computer (localhost, 127.0.0.1), and nothing else: to pages already open as you turn it on, and to those you open after. Turning it off stops that; a page still open keeps the entry until it's reloaded, and it refuses every call. While it's on, every https site you open, every page served from this computer, and the scripts on them, can see that you use Seedelf Wallet, even sites you never connect (not your addresses or balance until you connect).
- The extension never fetches JavaScript or WebAssembly to run: its code all ships in its package. The one program it takes from outside is a DEX order's script, read from the Cardano blockchain, which the wallet's own evaluator runs to check that it can cancel a swap's order. That evaluator gives the script no access to your browser, and runs it only if it matches the hash the package carries.

## Your control

- Everything the extension keeps is in your browser.
- Removing the extension deletes its storage, including the encrypted wallet. Keep your recovery phrase: it's the only way back in.
- The Chrome Web Store and Chrome itself know this extension is installed in your browser, and Chrome Sync, if it syncs your extensions, installs it in your other signed-in browsers. Only the extension is synced, never your wallet.
- While the wallet is unlocked, anyone using your browser can see your balances and history, and someone who knows Chrome's developer tools can read your keys. Lock the wallet (or close the browser) before leaving it.
