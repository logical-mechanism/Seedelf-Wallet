# Privacy

Seedelf Wallet is a Cardano wallet for Chrome with a private balance built in. This page is its privacy model: what it hides and from whom, what it can't hide, the rules it follows, and every link it knows of between parts of your money. It's for anyone deciding whether to trust the wallet with their money, or checking its claims.

The protocol underneath, stealth addresses and re-randomization, and the known attacks on it, are in the root README's [What is a Stealth Wallet?](../../../README.md#what-is-a-stealth-wallet) and [Wallet Limitations](../../../README.md#wallet-limitations). This page covers what the wallet does on top of them.

The sections from *In short* through *Rules the wallet enforces* are the model: read those first. *Known links* then goes through each feature in detail: what it links, what each service learns, and what's kept on the device. It's written to be looked up, a feature at a time.

## In short

If a word here is new, *The words on the screens*, just below, explains it.

- **Hidden, from people reading the chain:** who owns each UTxO in your private balance, and who spends it. Lovejoin, the mixer the wallet uses, also makes it harder to tell which box coming out of a mix is yours.
- **Not hidden:** amounts, tokens, timing and which transactions spend which outputs; everything your public account does, staking included; and every ordinary address that pays into the private balance or is paid from it.
- **Not hidden from the services that carry your transactions:** every transaction goes out through the Cardano server, and every private spend also goes to giveme.my, the collateral service. Both see your IP address, so either can tie your private payments and Lovejoin boxes to your public account. On mainnet, giveme.my and the server the wallet reads first have the same operator. A VPN hides who you are, not that the requests come from one place.
- **Settings that add privacy:** Lovejoin's longer waits (2 to 12 or 6 to 24 hours, instead of 1 to 6); four boxes a mix, so that at the default depth a box is one of up to 16 instead of 9; the currency set to *Nothing*, so CoinGecko is never asked; and connecting sites left off (the default), so no site can tell the wallet is installed.
- **The links to avoid:**
  - making money public back to where it came from;
  - a unique amount, or a payment right after you made the money private;
  - creating your Seedelf with money you made private yourself: create it from the public account first, then make money private;
  - spending money with different histories together (boxes back from Lovejoin with your own money, or two public accounts' money): the review, the screen you see before signing, says so when a payment does it;
  - paying one of your own public accounts from another;
  - bringing Lovejoin boxes back early, or all at once.
- **Few people use Seedelf and Lovejoin today,** so timing and amounts carry most of the risk.

## The words on the screens

- **The public account** is an ordinary Cardano account, the kind every Cardano wallet has. A recovery phrase can have several.
- **The private balance** is your money in Seedelf, the stealth wallet contract, where no UTxO says who owns it. There's one for the whole phrase.
- **Make private** moves money from the public account into the private balance. **Make public** pays from the private balance to any Cardano address. **Send** pays from either side.
- **A Seedelf** is an NFT that works like an ADA Handle for your private balance: give out its name, and anyone using Seedelf Wallet or the CLI can pay it without the payment showing it's yours. A payment to it sits under a *register*, a fresh, random-looking form of its owner's public key that people reading the chain can't link back to them.
- **A private session** is a one-time account, funded from the private balance, that a swap or a connected site uses as an ordinary wallet, and whose money comes back into the private balance afterwards.
- **Lovejoin** is a mixer on Cardano. It keeps pools of *boxes*, UTxOs that each hold the same amount, and the wallet can send your money through a pool so that each box comes out mixed with other people's: at the default settings, one of at most 9, and often fewer (*Lovejoin*, below). **Bootstrap** puts boxes in a pool unmixed, for other people to mix theirs with.
- **Collateral** is a UTxO that a transaction running a script must put up, which the network takes if the script fails. **giveme.my** is a collateral service that lends its own to private spends, so no collateral of yours appears in them.
- **Koios** is a public Cardano API. **The data layer** is the wallet's own Cardano server for mainnet, `mainnet.seedelf.logicalmechanism.io`. **The Cardano server** means whichever of the two the wallet is using to read the chain and send transactions: on mainnet, the data layer first and Koios for anything it can't answer; on preprod, Koios (*Network*, below).
- **Review** is the screen that shows a transaction before you sign and send it; a swap's is its approval.
- **The CLI** is Seedelf's command-line wallet, `seedelf-cli`, built on the same Rust code as this wallet.
- **Names in code font** are ones you can look up yourself: a Koios API request (such as `tx_info`, which the extension's network panel shows, and which the data layer answers too), a key's derivation path (such as `24301'/0/i`), or the name of something the wallet keeps in the browser's storage.

## How the wallet chooses

- **The most private choice is the default, with a control to turn it down where it costs fees, time or convenience.** The controls are in Settings (Lovejoin's switches, its depth, its waits and its mixes' width; the currency; connecting sites; *Read Cardano through Koios only*), and a return's review and a swap's approval each carry their own Lovejoin switch. A default never means the only choice.
- **Some settings go further than the default,** at a cost: Lovejoin's longer waits, four boxes a mix, and the currency set to *Nothing* (*In short*, above).
- **The wallet never forbids a link you choose.** It tells you what a payment or a connection reveals, and avoids making a link on your behalf, but it doesn't stop you making one.

## What is hidden and what isn't

**Hidden, from people reading the chain:**

- **Who owns a UTxO.** Registers are re-randomized, so they can't be linked back to a user. This relies on ECDDH, the elliptic-curve decisional Diffie–Hellman assumption.
- **Who spends it.** A spend proves ownership with a Schnorr proof, and carries a fresh one-time signing key and shared collateral.

**Visible:**

- **Amounts and tokens.** Seedelf doesn't hide or mix value.
- **The transaction graph and timing.**
- **Which ordinary addresses paid into the contract or received from it,** such as the public account and one-time accounts.
- **Everything the public account does,** staking included: its pool, where its vote goes, and its rewards.
- **Your IP address,** as seen by the Cardano server the wallet reads (*Network*, below), by giveme.my for every private spend, on mainnet by CoinGecko for ADA's price, by Minswap for a swap, and by Blockfrost's IPFS gateway for an NFT's image you ask to see.
- **To the Cardano server and giveme.my, much of what the chain hides** (*Network*, below): every transaction goes to the server from the IP address that reads your public account, and every private spend goes to giveme.my from it too. Seedelf and Lovejoin hide your money from people reading the chain, not from the services that carry your transactions.

## Rules the wallet enforces

These aren't settings: the wallet always follows them.

1. **A new one-time signing key for every private spend.**
   - The CLI draws it at random.
   - The wallet derives it inside WebAssembly from the Seedelf key and a fresh random seed, so it survives a restart of the extension's worker between Review and Send without ever reaching JavaScript. A new seed gives a new key.
2. **Private spends take their collateral from giveme.my, the shared service,** never from a UTxO of yours. Your own collateral would tag every private spend with your address.
   - **The one exception is a private session's return merged into its funding's change.** It puts up the session's own 5 ₳ collateral, which the same transaction spends. It tags nothing new: the transaction already spends the session's UTxOs.
   - Its proof is still bound to a new one-time key (rule 1), never the session's key, which a connected site can ask to sign.
3. **Re-randomization scalars (`d`, the random number a register is re-randomized with) are toxic waste:** anyone who learns one can turn that register back into its owner's.
   - They're full-size and come from a secure random source.
   - They're never stored or logged.
   - They exist only inside `seedelf-crypto`, the wallet's Rust cryptography library.
4. **Only `seedelf-crypto` builds a register,** never TypeScript. That's what makes sure the same `d` is applied to both points, and that the points are torsion-free (in the curve's prime-order subgroup): a payment to a register with any other point can't be spent. A mistake here locks money for good (the root README's [Wallet Limitations](../../../README.md#wallet-limitations)).
   - A payment to someone's Seedelf goes under a fresh re-randomization of their register, never the register as found.
   - The wallet refuses to pay a register whose points don't decode, lie outside the prime-order subgroup, or are the identity. A payment to any of those is locked for good, or anyone can take it.
5. **A Seedelf is linked to whatever pays for it, so the wallet has you create your first one before you make any money private.**
   - **By default the public account pays for it.** Until the wallet has a Seedelf and a private balance, Home lists the steps in that order: fund the public account, create a Seedelf, then make money private.
   - **Paying from the private balance is the other choice.** It hides the payer only when what it spends is a box back from Lovejoin or other people's payments, and whoever paid you still knows their own (*Creating a Seedelf*, below). Then the wallet passes only Seedelf UTxOs to WebAssembly, which refuses any it doesn't own, and its coin selection takes first a box that pays alone where the wallet knows it's mixed, then received money, then any other box (but one a deposit of yours put in, which goes as what paid for that deposit, and one whose way back money of a known history but another box paid, which goes as that money); what an earlier spend of yours left comes after those, and before money you made private unless it's tied to one of your accounts or private sessions as well (*Co-spending*, below).
6. **The public account is never a one-time account.** Each one-time account is used for a single session. One-time accounts use a reserved account index (`24301'`), never a low index like `1'` that a restored Lace wallet may already use: sharing payment keys with a real account would link every one-time address back to you.
7. **No analytics or telemetry.** The wallet talks to the Cardano server (on mainnet its own data layer first, *Network*, and Koios), to giveme.my, on mainnet to CoinGecko for ADA's price unless the currency is set to *Nothing*, to Minswap's aggregator only when you swap, and to Blockfrost's IPFS gateway only when you ask to see an NFT's image, and to nothing else.
   - Every request goes without cookies or a referrer (`credentials: "omit"`, `referrerPolicy: "no-referrer"`), and none is kept in Chrome's cache on the disk (`cache: "no-store"`).
   - **One exception, for the data layer's requests that send it something** (POSTs, `cache: "default"`): Before such a request, browsers check that the server accepts it (a CORS preflight), and Chrome remembers the data layer's answer in memory only, for up to 2 hours, keyed by each request's address, never by what it sends. Such an address can carry a block height the wallet last read and, past 1,000 rows, an outpoint of the account's. The data layer answers every request with `Cache-Control: no-store`, so nothing it says is kept. A plain read (a GET) needs no such check and stays `no-store`: its address can name an ADA Handle or a DRep ID, and `no-store` keeps that off the disk whatever a server says.
   - **giveme.my and the data layer have the same operator.** giveme.my sees the IP address with every private spend and with the one Lovejoin mix of a private session's 5 ₳ collateral, and the data layer sees what Koios would (*Network*). Koios, CoinGecko, Minswap and Blockfrost are third parties.
   - **CoinGecko is the one default that asks a third party for a convenience:** the currency is US dollars unless you change it. *Nothing* asks no one.
   - **Lovejoin is a contract,** reached through the Cardano server: nothing goes to it as a service. giveme.my sees each box brought back, as with any private spend, and a private session's collateral mix too.
   - The wallet talks only to the network you've chosen, except to finish something already under way on the other one.
8. **A site is given the public account, or a private session's one-time account, never the private balance.** The Seedelf key and the Seedelfs are never offered over CIP-30. A private session's funding is public, though, and leads back into the private balance (*A site connected to a private session*). Connecting sites is off until you turn it on, and until then nothing is added to any web page.

## Known links

The wallet can't prevent these links. Wherever it can tell one is about to happen, it says so on that screen.

### Making money private

- **Make private links the public account, and whoever funded it, to the private UTxOs it creates.** For a restored Lace or Yoroi phrase, the public account is your public identity.
- On its own it doesn't link to a Seedelf's name: from outside it looks the same as paying someone else's Seedelf.

### Creating a Seedelf

A Seedelf is linked to whatever pays for it.

- **Paid from the private balance, out of money you made private yourself:** the creation spends that money and returns the change in the same transaction. That links the public account, the name and the remaining balance.
- **Paid by the public account, then Make private:** only the account and the name are linked. The balance stays ambiguous. This is the default.
- **Paid from the private balance, out of a box back from Lovejoin or money you received:** hides the payer from people reading the chain. That's the case paying from the private balance is for. No payer knows a box back from a mix, but one who put it in under your key and mixed it themselves (*Lovejoin*), so a box the wallet knows is mixed goes first when it pays alone, then money you received, then any other box but one a deposit of yours put in, or one whose way back money of a known history but another box paid (*Co-spending*).
- **But whoever paid you knows that UTxO.** They can tie, in public, the Seedelf it pays for to the Seedelf they paid, and a payment made under a copy of a register already on chain (d = 1) shows that to everyone (*Co-spending*). The same holds for a session's funding or a pool's start paid with it.
- **Without a box or money you received that pays, what an earlier private spend of yours left goes before money you made private.** It ties the new Seedelf, in the open, to that spend (an earlier Seedelf's creation, say), not to your public account. Change that's tied to one of your accounts or private sessions already (what a Make public to the account left, or change of money you made private) goes with that money, or after it. But a Make public an earlier version sent noted no account it paid: unless it spent money tied to an account or a session already, what it left goes before money you made private, as change, even where it paid one of your own accounts.

### Making money public

- **Make public to where the money came from re-links the chain.** This is the second implicit tracking method in the root README's [Implicit Tracking Methods](../../../README.md#implicit-tracking-methods). Make it public somewhere else, or keep it private.
- **The Make public form says so.** It warns when the destination is **any of this wallet's own public accounts**: an address under one of their payment keys, whatever its staking part. It checks the first 20 addresses of each chain; for the account the wallet is on, any its last balance reading found; and up to the 500th of each chain when the address carries that account's own stake key, as another wallet with the same phrase may hand out. Every account the wallet knows is checked, not only the active one: the money is re-linked to that account either way, and the other accounts' keys are derived on the device, asking no one. Such a payment links the account to the private UTxOs spent, and so to whoever made them private.
- **A stake key alone doesn't make an address yours:** anyone can pair their own payment key with it. Such an address gets a warning of its own: only that payment key's holder can spend the money, and anyone can still link the address to this money and to whoever made it private.
- **Max** spends every UTxO, whatever its history, including bundles of tokens that add no ADA, so it ties them all together (*Co-spending*). It spends 20 a transaction, in as many transactions as it needs, starting with those that add the most ADA (what each holds, less what its own tokens need). The review says which histories it merges.

### A payment in parts

- **A payment too large for one transaction** (more UTxOs than one spends, or more than 16 KiB) goes as several transactions to the same recipient.
- That ties every UTxO they spend together, as one transaction spending them all would: nothing new. The review names every history the parts spend between them, not only those one part merges.
- Parts that pay their own fee go one right after another.
- A part with no ADA of its own to pay with (bundles of tokens being made private, say) spends what the part before carried forward: an output of ADA alone back into the private balance. So the chain shows the parts are one payer's, as they were anyway. The carry's history is the part before's (*Co-spending*).
- A carry no later part spends stays in the private balance as change does, and the review counts it as change: a funding's note names it among the private change tied to the one-time account.
- giveme.my witnesses only inputs on the ledger, so such a part waits until the one before is in a block, and the wallet sends it then, while it's unlocked (*Network*). A lock stops the rest, and what they'd have spent stays in the private balance.
- Each part asks giveme.my and the Cardano server as any private spend does.

### Sending from the public account

- **It's an ordinary Cardano payment:** anyone can see it came from the account, and so from whoever funded it. The Send form says so, and that paying from the private balance instead avoids the link.
- **To someone's Seedelf,** it looks like Make private from outside: the account paid into the wallet contract, under a fresh re-randomization of the recipient's register, so nothing on chain says whose Seedelf it is. The recipient can see it came from your account. The form says both.
- **To your own Seedelf,** it is a Make private, beside the other recipients: the money goes into your private balance, linked to the account as Make private's is, and the wallet notes it as made private from that account (*Co-spending*), never as money someone paid you. The form and the review say it's yours.

### Removing a Seedelf

- **Its ADA goes somewhere, and that's linked to the Seedelf's name.**
- **The default follows who paid for it:** the public account for a Seedelf the account paid for, which is linked to it already, and the private balance for one the private balance paid for.
- The wallet records who paid when a creation is sent, naming the public account its review was built and signed on, even if another window has switched account by then. The record is sealed, per network (`mintedBy.<network>`), and kept apart from the Seedelf history, so trimming that history never loses it.
- For a Seedelf with no such record here (one created in another browser, say), it works out who paid from what it holds, never asking the Cardano server about the creation: another of its contract UTxOs from the creating transaction is the change of a creation the private balance paid for; an Activity entry in which the account spent marks one the account paid for. A payment to the account in that transaction doesn't, since anyone can create a Seedelf under your register and pay you beside it. Otherwise nothing is chosen, and Review waits for you.
- Each side's note says what it links for this Seedelf, with a warning where it links something new. To the public account, a Seedelf the private balance paid for ties its name, and the creation's private inputs and change, to the account. Into the private balance, one the account paid for ties the name to that new UTxO, and to whatever it's later spent with.
- With several public accounts, it names the account that paid (*Several public accounts*).

### Amounts and timing

- **Unique amounts and timing are an easy match:** making 1,234.567 ADA private and making about 1,234.4 ADA public an hour later. Round amounts, and not rushing, make it harder. The Send form says that sending right after Make private is easy to link by timing.

### Sending from the private balance

- **The payment can't be linked to the recipient's Seedelf.** The payer's side is an ordinary spend, though.
- Its inputs, and the change in the same transaction, trace back through the transaction graph to where that money came from.
- If you made the money private yourself, the chain leads from your public account to this payment, though not to your Seedelf's name, nor to the recipient's.
- Its amount and timing are public. Sending right after Make private is easy to match by timing, and the form says so.
- **Its outputs are shuffled** (Fisher–Yates over the operating system's random source), so the change isn't always last. A round payment next to non-round change still shows which output is the payment.
- Paying your own Seedelf is allowed, but it only moves money in a circle.

### Paying several recipients at once

- **Paying several recipients in one transaction shows they were paid together, by one payer.**
- From the public account that payer is you, in the open. From the private balance it isn't, but the addresses a Make public pays together are tied to each other. Payments to Seedelfs stay unlinkable to their Seedelfs either way.
- The Send and Make public forms say so once there's a second recipient.

### Co-spending

Spending several UTxOs in one transaction suggests they share an owner. Coin selection keeps money with different histories apart where it can, in what pays beside the UTxOs holding a token you send, which go first whatever made them (*Selection*, below).

- **Each private UTxO has a history,** worked out on the device from the sealed Seedelf history by the transaction that made it, never by asking the Cardano server:
  - made private from a public account: one history per account (*Several public accounts*), what a site's transaction you sign pays into your own Seedelf from that account included, noted as you sign it, since the site may send it itself;
  - each payment received: one history each;
  - each box back from Lovejoin: one each. Where the wallet knows it's mixed (its own chain's last mix made it, or the Cardano server said a mix did), it's marked so: the box a Seedelf takes first. One the wallet knows a deposit of yours put where it is, with no mix since, carries what paid for that deposit, that public account's money or that private session's, and a deposit's box is never marked as mixed. One a chain of yours left short of its last mixes is a box alone. Any other, one someone else put in under your key among them, or one whose making the wallet can't tell, is a box tagged Unknown too. A Seedelf, or any other session's funding, takes all of these after money others paid, but one whose way back other money paid: like any spend's output, it carries that money's history too, so one a payment received paid goes as that payment (*Lovejoin*). One an earlier version brought back keeps what that version noted, most often the box alone;
  - all of a private session's money (what each funding and top-up left, its return, and what a site on it pays your own Seedelf): one per session, carrying the histories of what paid for each funding, a box or a payment received, so a later review's note counts those too;
  - or Unknown.
- **A spend's change keeps its inputs' histories,** merged, **and the tie the spend made:** what a Seedelf's creation, a pool's start, a Send, a Make public or a removal leaves in the private balance is tied to that spend in the open (a Make public to one of your own accounts, to that account). So, where one UTxO pays alone, a Seedelf, a pool's start or a funding paid later takes money others paid you, any box but one from a deposit of yours, and Unknown money before it, and it before money you made private; change that also names one of your accounts or a private session goes after that money. Change whose history an earlier version kept gets its spend's tie when the history is read, asking no one, but where that history names one of your accounts. An earlier version's Make public noted no account it paid, so what it left gets that spend's tie, even where it paid one of yours. Unknown money is one history only while none of the UTxOs a spend could take has a known one; once any has, each transaction's Unknown money is a history of its own.
- **Selection tries not to merge histories.** A token you send is paid first from the UTxOs holding it, those holding the most of it first, whatever made them, a box back from Lovejoin among them (a chain of parts with more of them than one part spends takes those adding ADA beyond their tokens' least first, then by history; where that order can't plan the chain, a session's funding takes them by history first, and a Send or Make public by what each adds): the order below is for what pays beside them. It pays from one UTxO alone, or from UTxOs of one history, before it spends several histories together, and when it must merge, boxes back from Lovejoin come last, counted by what each pays, a token pool's way back with its token among them (in one transaction, what it pays beside the change the spend leaves anyway, the tokens the other money and the UTxOs holding one you send leave in it counted; in a payment's parts, its ADA less the least its token needs back as change): of the choices it tries, one tagged Unknown too only where none pays without one, even where that merges more boxes, and then with as few others as pay; else as few boxes as pay; and of as many, the choice with the most the wallet knows are mixed, but never one that merges more of the other money. Each box is counted by itself, beside the change as the spend foresees it, so it can be off by its token's share of that change's least: about 0.2 ₳ where the token joins an output of change with room, a little more for a long token name, and up to an output's whole least, about 1.6 ₳, where the change's last output is full, or in a payment's parts where boxes' tokens share one (two boxes holding one token pay it once). Within that, a merge can take more boxes than pay, or fewer the wallet knows are mixed, and where it takes one tagged Unknown too, more of them than pay; and near the most one transaction can spend, or in a payment's parts, beside boxes holding tokens, one tagged Unknown too where a choice it doesn't try pays without one (two ways back of one pool, say, or a token that shares an output of change or opens one). Boxes still come after the other money where one transaction can't spend all of it, so a session's funding takes another session's money before a box where that pays. None of that refuses, or sends in parts, a spend it would otherwise send as one transaction: where none of its own choices can go, it falls back to the orders it would otherwise take, and before any merge of its own that takes a box tagged Unknown too, it tries those without any. Either way, a box a deposit of yours put in goes as what paid for that deposit, and one whose way back money of a known history but another box paid, as that money. Which money it tries first depends on the spend: Send and Make public take money you made private first, and boxes last, where one pays alone one the wallet knows is mixed first, then any other, one tagged Unknown too after the rest; a Seedelf or a pool's start paid from the private balance takes first a box that pays alone where the wallet knows it's mixed, then received money, then any other box, Unknown money, what an earlier spend left and money you made private, and a private session's money last; a session's funding takes received money, then a box the wallet knows is mixed, then any other box, one tagged Unknown too after the other boxes, then Unknown money, what an earlier spend left and money you made private, and another session's money last; a top-up takes its own session's money first. A payment in parts picks by history the same way, and Max spends every UTxO either way. The search, step by step, is in [architecture.md](architecture.md#transaction-building).
- **It never refuses a payment the CLI's order would make, and it doesn't stop to ask you.** When nothing it tried without merging pays (the change must exist, or the payment is large), it merges, and the review says what's spent together in a plain note ("This spends 2 boxes back from Lovejoin and money you made private together. Anyone can see they're one owner's, which ties them to each other…"). The search is best effort, so the note never says nothing else could have paid. It names a spend's change as change from an earlier spend, and a funding also names money another session left. There's no switch to turn it off.
- **A UTxO that adds less than nothing goes last:** one whose tokens need more ADA kept with them than it holds, such as a stranger's pile of tokens sent at their least. Every spend takes it after everything else, boxes included: only for a token in it you're sending, when nothing else pays, or with Max, which spends every UTxO. So a payment that doesn't need it never shows its sender which other UTxOs are yours, nor holds your ADA back with its tokens. The CLI picks the same way.
- **The UTxOs screen tags each UTxO** (Back from Lovejoin, Received, Made private, Private session N, Change, Unknown; one can carry several), so locking one by hand is an informed choice. With more than one public account it says which: *Made private (account 2)*. That tag also marks what a Make public to that account left, which the payment ties to it (one an earlier version sent noted no account, so its change is tagged as any other spend's), and *Back from Lovejoin* alone doesn't say whether the wallet knows the box is mixed. **A locked UTxO** stays out of every spend, Max included.
- **Whoever paid you knows that UTxO,** and can tie, in public, whatever it later pays for (a Seedelf, a session's funding, a pool's start) to the Seedelf they paid. A payment made under a copy of a register already on chain (d = 1) shows that to everyone. So a UTxO under a register another of your private UTxOs or Seedelfs already has is never counted as a payment received: it takes that one's history, with Unknown in place of any box back from Lovejoin it names, or is Unknown where that one is a payment received, and those spends take it after money paid properly. That catches only such a copy, never a payer who gives away another d.
- **After a restore,** or with the phrase in another browser, this device starts a history of its own, and marks whatever its first reading finds as Unknown, not received: Activity calls it *Already in your private balance*, and the UTxOs screen tags it Unknown. While none of them has a known history, they're all one Unknown history, and selection picks as the CLI does (pure ADA, largest first). A new wallet's first reading finds nothing, so nothing changes for it. The history keeps every entry whose money is still in the private balance, so a box still held never turns Unknown. **With the phrase in another Chrome profile,** what that profile later sends from the private balance comes back here as money in: on mainnet, where the data layer names who spent each UTxO, one that spent this device's UTxOs is *Already in your private balance*, with their history, and a funding takes it last, as another session's money; a box it brought back, or anything read through Koios (*Read Cardano through Koios only*, the data layer down, or preprod), reads as a payment received, and is spent as one.
- **What it can't fix:** a payment above one box's worth, from money that is all Lovejoin boxes, has to merge boxes, and largely undoes the mix. So does a payment of part of one box from a pool whose box holds too little to pay out part of itself on its own: what comes back is spent with other money. With one private UTxO, every session chains to the last; only splitting it, or mixing it first (the Lovejoin tile, at its fees), avoids that. A session funded from money you made private yourself traces back to your public account through its funding, as a Send does.

### Several public accounts

A phrase can have more than one public account (CIP-1852 accounts), and the wallet works on one at a time. Some people keep several accounts *as a form of privacy*, so the wallet shouldn't link them on its own.

- **The wallet tells you about these links; it doesn't forbid them.** Accounts may be linked or not, and some of what the wallet does links them: Make private, Make public back to an account, a Seedelf an account paid for. So nothing below forbids a link: selection *prefers* not to make one, and every screen that could make one says what it would reveal.
- **There's one private balance for the whole phrase,** with the Seedelf key on account 0, and that costs nothing, because **stealth addressing already unlinks what each account makes private**: two accounts paying the same Seedelf create re-randomized registers `(g^d1, u^d1)` and `(g^d2, u^d2)`, which can't be tied to each other or back to `(g, u)` without `d`.
- **What that doesn't cover is spending together.** A contract UTxO's creating transaction is public: the registers hide *who* the money went to, not where an input came from. One private spend taking a UTxO made private from account 0 together with one from account 1 **ties those two accounts to one owner, in the open**. So each account's money made private is its own history, selection keeps them apart where a choice that doesn't merge them pays, and the review names the accounts when it can't: *"It spends money you made private from accounts 1 and 2, so anyone can tie those accounts to each other."*
- **Finding accounts asks the Cardano server about one account at a time.** A single `account_addresses` request for twenty stake addresses would tell the server those twenty are one wallet's. The requests go separately instead, but **the server still sees them from one IP address seconds apart**, so this is less linkage, not none. It runs at a restore and when you ask, never at unlock.
- **Reading is one account at a time, too.** The wallet never reads two accounts in one go, and never shows a total across them, so neither the server nor the screen puts them together on its own. You can still link them whenever you choose. **One exception, from the same IP address:** while a connected site uses a public account other than the one on screen, the site's reads ask about that account and the screens about the other, so the server sees both from one IP address, every 30 s while a site polls.
- **Lovejoin keeps them apart too:** each mix from the public side records the account that paid it, and *Mix again from my public account* takes only the active account's boxes. Another account's are named on the Lovejoin page, to mix again from that account. A mix record that names no account reads as account 0's. *Pay from my private balance anyway* still takes every account's, after its warning.
- **Paying one of your own accounts from another is allowed, and said.** It's an ordinary Cardano payment: anyone can see the two accounts paying each other and tell they're one wallet's. The Send form names the account and says so, and you decide, as with every other known link. So is paying someone else's address that carries another of your accounts' stake keys: on chain it reads as a payment to that account, so Send's warning says paying it still ties your two accounts together. It reads the resolved address, so Contacts and ADA Handles read the same.
  - **The wallet doesn't refuse it:** people do move money between their own accounts, and the wallet can't prevent these links, only show them. Treating accounts as though they were certainly unlinked would be wrong too: several things the wallet does link them, sometimes on purpose.
  - **The Send form offers them** (*Your accounts*, beside *Contacts*), which puts the link one click away, so the picker carries the note too. Without it you'd switch account, copy the address, switch back and paste: no more private, and much harder to use. The addresses are derived on the device, so opening the picker asks nobody anything. **Make public offers them too**, and there the picker's note says anyone can link the account picked to this money and to whoever made it private.
- **A custom account is reached by its number, and adding one asks nobody anything.** Checking an account by number asks the server about that one stake address, the same leak as finding accounts; **adding** it makes no request at all, so a fresh custom-numbered account tells the server nothing until you use it.
- **One account is the dApp account, and connected sites always use it** (Settings → Sites), whichever account the wallet is working on. So switching accounts shows a site nothing new, and no site is refused for being on the "wrong" one: the choice is made once, on purpose, rather than inferred from wherever the picker happens to be. Changing it shows every connected site the new account, which anyone watching both can see is the same wallet, and Settings says so.
- **Removing a Seedelf names the account that paid for it.** The creation is public, so it already links the Seedelf to that account; sending the freed ADA to a *different* one links that account to the same name, and anyone can join the two through it. The record keeps which account paid (one that names none means account 0), and Remove **chooses nothing** for you, and **warns** rather than letting a default do it quietly, under the cards and on the public account's card too: *"Account 1 · Savings paid for this Seedelf. Sending its ADA to Account 2 lets anyone tie the two accounts together…"* An account is named by its number and, once renamed, its name, since the picker shows a renamed account by its name alone.
- **What's on the disk unsealed:** which account is active, one integer (`seedelf.account`), beside the network and for the same reason: the keys are derived from it before anything is unlocked. So is which account connected sites use (`dappAccount`), among the settings, which aren't sealed. How many accounts the phrase has, and what they're called, are sealed with the other private records.
- **Each account's staking is its own** (its own `2/0` key), so stake is spread across however many accounts are used. That's public, as all staking is.

### A one-time account's stake key

- **Each private session's one-time account has its own stake key,** `24301'/2/i` beside its payment key `24301'/0/i`, which the wallet never registers: a site on the session asking for it is refused.
- **Some things need no signature of that key, though:** an old-style stake registration; a stake pool's registration that makes it the pool's reward account; a governance proposal whose deposit goes back to it; a treasury withdrawal that pays it; and an output paid to its hash. So a site on another account can put one in a transaction it asks the wallet to sign. The prompt then names the session, and warns that signing ties it to the account the site sees.
- A registration's deposit, paid by that account, then stays under the session's key, which nothing in the wallet deregisters: the session's return and Disconnect read the UTxOs under its payment key only. So would a proposal's deposit once its action ends, and the prompt says so: it goes to the session's stake key, and the wallet never brings it back. So does what a treasury withdrawal pays that key, and an output paid to its hash, whose row reads "An address": the return, Disconnect and the restore scan never read either.
- No two sessions share a key, so nothing in the address ties one session to another, or to anyone else.
- **A SundaeSwap V3 or V4 order's cancel is signed by it:** the order names the stake key as its owner. The signature shows the key itself, whose hash the address and the order already show, so it ties nothing new. A V3 order sits under Minswap's staking part, the same for every sender, not the session's; a V4 order under the session's own.
- **The earliest sessions, made on preprod before any release, sit under the shared Seedelf staking hash** instead (the CLI's External Wallet's), which ties them together. They keep that address, since that's where their money is. No mainnet session uses it.

### The restore scan

- **The restore scan asks about the one-time accounts together.** After a restore, or from **Find leftovers** on the dApps page, the wallet asks the Cardano server about the one-time accounts in batches of 20, then about the used ones' keys and their transactions, all from one IP address within seconds. So the server can tell those sessions are one wallet's, which their accounts' own keys never tie on chain, and the requests come from the IP address that reads the public account. Each request is listed in [keys-and-accounts.md](keys-and-accounts.md#accounts).
- It also asks about the first contract UTxO that each other payment into those accounts spent, strangers' payments too. The server has just listed those inputs in the transactions it gave, so this tells it nothing new; which payments were this phrase's is worked out on the device.
- **The batches name the sessions to come too.** The scan always asks past the last used account, so it names the accounts the next sessions will take: 20 for a phrase that never used one, 20 to 39 for one that did. Once those sessions are funded, the server can tie them to the scan's requests, whatever IP address they're opened from, once a scan. It stays because a scan that stops at a gap has to ask past the last used account, and each of those sessions goes through the same server, from the IP address that reads the public account, anyway (*A new session's index*, below).
- On mainnet that server is the wallet's own data layer, which logs neither the address nor what's asked. With it down, with *Read Cardano through Koios only* on (which Remove wallet and Forgot password keep, so a restore after them asks Koios alone), or on preprod, it's Koios.
- **That's why it runs only at a restore** (on the network restored on) **or when asked,** never as the wallet opens. Find leftovers' privacy note says so before it's pressed.
- **At a restore it runs by itself, and the Restore screen doesn't say so beforehand.** The privacy policy does. In the wallet, only notes afterwards tell of it: Home's, when the scan found sessions or didn't finish, and the dApps page's while a network's look hasn't finished, the other network's included. Once it has looked and found nothing, nothing says it did.
- Each session's funding already ties it to the private UTxOs that paid it, on chain: the scan adds the server's view of them all together, nothing on chain.
- **Found sessions come back by hand,** each in its own transaction, as Bring everything back's do. Some first need 5 ₳ put in from the private balance, for an order's cancel or for Lovejoin's mixes. That payment takes the session's own private money first where some is left, which ties nothing new; otherwise it links the private UTxOs it spends to the session's account, as its funding did, and its review says so.
- Remove wallet and Forgot password say the phrase brings back what private sessions' accounts hold, not what a site's session left open at the site, like a listing or an order the wallet can't cancel.

### Crowd size

- **Privacy grows with the number of honest users** (the flood attack, in the root README's [Implicit Tracking Methods](../../../README.md#implicit-tracking-methods)). With few users, timing and amounts carry most of the risk. The wallet's Lovejoin notes say a box hides among fewer while few people use Lovejoin.

### Network

**The Cardano server and giveme.my see your IP address.**

- **On mainnet the server is the wallet's own data layer first:** `mainnet.seedelf.logicalmechanism.io`, on a DigitalOcean droplet in New York. It reads its own Cardano node and indexes (db-sync and Kupo), on another machine, over a private link (WireGuard), so that machine never sees a user.
  - Each part (the public routes, the private index, submits) goes to Koios on its own for 5 minutes when it can't answer. A request too large for it (a credential or an address with more than 20,000 unspent outputs, transactions gathered from more than 200,000 outputs, or a page of transactions more than 10,000 in) goes to Koios, that request alone, with no hold.
  - Settings → Network's *Read Cardano through Koios only* turns it off, and Remove wallet and Forgot password keep that switch, as they keep the network. Preprod reads Koios alone.
  - **So on mainnet, "the Cardano server" on this page means whichever of the two answered.**
- **What's different about the data layer:**
  - **The public side and submits** tell it what they'd tell Koios: the stake address, the transactions sent, from the IP address. The same operator runs giveme.my, so it sees both a private spend's collateral request and its submit.
  - **The private side tells it less.** The wallet reads every UTxO at the contract: a snapshot at a restore, then only what changed since a sealed cursor, and every wallet at that cursor gets the same answer. After an update that changes how the wallet reads its rows or its Lovejoin boxes, its first read asks a snapshot again, as a restore does, and its next pool read reads each pool copy it keeps once more (*A copy holding the wallet's boxes*, under *Lovejoin*), the same for every updated device. Seedelf names come as one shared list. Lovejoin's pool comes with what made every box, so while the data layer is answering, the wallet doesn't need to ask what made its own boxes in that pool. While the wallet holds the private index on Koios, it asks Koios alone about them, never the data layer. The data layer learns roughly when the wallet last read (the cursor), and while a private transaction is watched on the feed, that it's polled every 5 s. It never learns a UTxO, a register, a box or an owner from those reads.
  - **What it keeps:** its web server, Caddy, has no access log, and neither Caddy's own log nor the API's has a client address or a path. The per-IP rate limits live in memory alone. That's a policy, published with the code, not a proof.
  - **A submitted transaction's first hop** is a stake pool relay run by the same operator, whose registration publishes its IP address.
  - **Its name is seen on your network:** every reading's DNS lookup and TLS handshake name `mainnet.seedelf.logicalmechanism.io`, so an internet provider, a Wi-Fi's owner or a DNS resolver can tell the device runs Seedelf Wallet on mainnet, and when. Koios's name says only "some Cardano tool"; giveme.my's says Seedelf too, but only at a private spend.
- **The screens say "the server":** on mainnet it's usually the data layer, on preprod Koios. Settings → Network says on mainnet which answers first, and the privacy policy names both. Koios has no onion service, and whether it takes Tor exits is unchecked, so the wallet doesn't suggest Tor. A VPN helps with some of it; see the root README's [De-Anonymizing Via IP Tracking](../../../README.md#de-anonymizing-via-ip-tracking).
- **Sending is different from reading.** The Cardano server sends every transaction the wallet makes: private spends, session steps, Lovejoin chains and each box brought back, a site's submits, and a status check (`tx_status`) for each. A submit names the UTxOs it spends, and it comes from the IP address that reads your public account. So the server, and giveme.my for private spends, can tie your private payments, your sessions and your Lovejoin boxes to your public account, both ends of a Lovejoin trip included. A VPN hides who you are, not that the requests come from one place. Only a server you run yourself would take that out, and the wallet can't use one yet. The wallet's timing (nothing sent at the moment of unlock, the private side read alone after a private spend) helps only on an IP address others share.
- **giveme.my sees every private transaction.** Shared collateral means it must see each one to check and sign it: every private spend (each part of a payment or a funding in parts), a session's funding, each box brought back from Lovejoin, and the one mix of a session's 5 ₳ collateral, which names the session's one-time account (*Lovejoin*), with the IP address, before it's on chain. From one IP address it can group them as one person's, including a session's funding with its collateral's mix and its Lovejoin boxes coming back. Lovejoin and the random waits hide your boxes from people watching the chain, not from the services that carry them.
  - Its logs leave out raw IP addresses and, on success, transaction hashes; its rate limiter keeps IP addresses briefly. Switching VPN server between a mix and its boxes' return helps.
  - It stays the one collateral provider: your own would put your UTxO in every private spend.
  - Every review that asks it says so where it's read: who runs it, that it lends 5 ₳ of its own and charges nothing for it (its fixed UTxO is the collateral, the return goes back to it, and nothing in the transaction pays it), and that it sees the transaction with the IP address. Its refusals name it, rather than guessing at your money.
  - A session's collateral's mix asks it later, by itself, and the reviews say so first (*The session's 5 ₳ collateral*, under *Lovejoin*).
- **No Koios API key:** the wallet uses Koios's public tier, so there's no key that names who you are. The requests themselves name the public account's stake address. A key would tie every request to whoever registered it.
- **A balance reading** asks the Cardano server about the public account and the whole wallet contract at the same moment (between full reads, the part of it after the last block seen). The server can tell that the account's owner uses Seedelf, though not, from the reading, which contract UTxOs are theirs: the ownership check runs in the extension, on every row. That lasts until one is spent: the spend names it (above).
- **When the wallet reads:**
  - The balances only when Home opens (at most once a minute), or on Refresh.
  - The public account's Activity only while Activity is open: `credential_txs` by the account's payment keys, which the balance reading already sent, one request for every 75, and one `tx_info` for each page of 20. A Load more that finds only transactions listed already reads on in the same press, up to ten pages, at most one more request a group for each. The private Activity asks no one, since it's kept on the device.
  - While unlocked, a one-minute alarm reads only what's running: a swap's account and its orders, from the Cardano server, never Minswap (where a Splash order was filled in part, also the transactions that left the rest at its address, `tx_info`, each asked once); a Lovejoin chain being sent; a box that's due; a payment that may still go through (looked for each minute, sent again every two minutes); a box brought back that may have gone through (looked for each minute, for up to two hours); and a payment's parts that wait for the one before (that part looked for at most every 15 s, on the contract's feed where the data layer has one, `tx_status` otherwise, and the next sent once it shows, through giveme.my and the Cardano server as Send sends one; never in the unlock's run, and Home's poll does the same while it's open).
  - At unlock, Lovejoin's pool is read once on each network where the wallet has something open there. Bring every box back now, or a pool's, reads its pools at each run while it goes; once it stops, if it sent anything, the alarm reads them once more 2 hours on, then again 2 hours on while one of its withdraws may not be on chain yet. For the run that brings the retired pools' boxes back, where it began by letting go an earlier version's Bootstrap there or its count of boxes not mixed yet, which have no wait of their own, that read comes at the next run once it's over or stopped, whatever it sent, instead of 2 hours on (*The retired pools*, below). The read at unlock and those owed after a stop wait while a mix or a box's way back of the wallet's is being sent, and the unlock's is then skipped.
  - A pool read that finds more boxes than the wallet has due times for (after a restore, say) also asks Koios what made the boxes no record accounts for (`tx_info`), each transaction once, and every later pool read that counts the boxes (the Lovejoin page's, each time it opens and at Refresh, and again once a mix, a Bootstrap, Mix my boxes again or a pool's start is sent, a review is left, a Bring one back press or Bring them all back goes through or asks its question again, or a run of Bring every box back a pool's page shows ends or stops, never after its other buttons nor a press that fails; each Bring one back press's, but one refused at once; the unlock's; and the alarm's, at a box's due time and in each read owed above, after a run of Bring every box back stops, or the retired pools' run is over or stopped; never those runs' own reads while they go, nor a review's) asks again about one of those Koios hasn't answered for, with, while one is held for that answer with no due time, the pool's other boxes no record or answer names; and taking a box of unknown origin asks it first, and again at the next take where Koios didn't answer (*Lovejoin*).
  - While a payment is on its way, Home asks for its status every 15 s.
  - While a mix from the public account that stopped at a transaction that may have gone through is unsettled, it's looked for (`tx_status`, then `utxo_info` of what it spends) at most every two minutes on each network while the worker runs. The throttle is in the worker's memory, so a worker the browser started again, as it may for each of the alarm's runs with no page open, looks at once. It's looked for by the one-minute alarm, with no page open and at the unlock too; by Home (every 20 s while it's open), the UTxOs page and the Lovejoin page, on the network shown; and its Review and Send look each time. After the same phrase is restored, one Remove wallet kept is looked for the same way.
  - Locking stops all of it, so a VPN must stay on for as long as the wallet is unlocked with something running.
- **When a private spend lands, the wallet reads only the private side again:** the contract from the last block seen, not the public account, so the server doesn't see the account read in the second the private transaction lands. So do a box brought back from Lovejoin, a session's return and a private session's site sending a transaction; a swap, a cancel or a chain's mix reads neither again. What touches the account (Make private, a public send, a Make public or a removal to it, staking, a mix from the public account, a public site's transaction) and Refresh still read both.
- **Hiding the balances** only changes what's drawn: the wallet reads the same and asks no one anything more. It hides how many Lovejoin boxes the wallet holds too, since every box of a pool holds the same.
- **Reviewing a private spend tells the server nothing new:** its scripts are measured in the wallet. Only Send submits it. Two reviews still go to the server to be measured (by Ogmios, which runs a transaction's scripts against a Cardano node): a Seedelf paid for by the public account, whose draft shows the server the Seedelf's tag and the account's inputs, and a Lovejoin chain's first mix, with its unsent deposit.
- **Token names and logos come from a list inside the extension,** refreshed at each release. Asking the server about the tokens in the private balance would tell it which contract UTxOs are yours, so the wallet never does on its own. The one exception is yours: **Show image** on an NFT asks the server about that one token (*An NFT's image*), and says so first.
- **Finding a recipient** uses the contract as the balance reading sees it, and picks the Seedelf's UTxO in the extension. The wallet never asks the server about the recipient's token (`asset_utxos` and the like): that would tell it exactly who is being paid.
- **An ADA Handle can't be found that way:** Make public or Send to `$name` asks the server who holds that handle (`asset_nft_address`), so it learns it. The transaction names the address anyway once it's submitted. Pasting the address instead asks nothing.
- **Cardanoscan links** open in the browser's own profile, and only when clicked: Cardanoscan then sees the IP address, its own cookies and what was opened, and the browser keeps it in its history (Google's too, with History sync). On a private transaction (the private Activity, Home's banner for a private kind or a Seedelf paid from the private balance, a swap's steps, a session's funding, a site session's account) the link says so: "Opening this tells Cardanoscan and your browser history that this transaction is yours".
- **Settings' links** (the source code, the privacy policy, *Report a translation error*) open GitHub in the browser's own profile, and only when clicked: GitHub then sees the IP address and its own cookies, and an issue filed there is public, under the GitHub account that files it.

### On this device

- **Which contract UTxOs are yours** is kept in memory and in `chrome.storage.session`, whose copy is wiped on lock, and on mainnet sealed with the vault in the `contract.mainnet` record (`chrome.storage.local`, with the private index's cursor), so an unlock asks only what changed. Never on disk unencrypted.
- **Contacts** (who you pay), the Seedelf history, and **the UTxOs you locked** (with the collateral chosen, and the transactions that set it by payment, for Activity to name) are kept on disk, but **never unencrypted**: sealed under a key derived from the phrase, unreadable while locked, deleted with the wallet. Saving or checking a contact, or locking a UTxO, asks no one anything.
- **A payment being sent** is sealed on disk the same way from just before it goes to the server: the signed transaction, the UTxOs it spends and what it pays. Once the server takes it, or refuses it outright, the record is deleted. One the server didn't answer may still go through, and its record stays until it lands or can't any more, then is deleted. So a lock, a closed browser or a stopped worker can't let a second payment go out beside it. A box brought back from Lovejoin is sealed the same way before it's sent, in `lovejoin.<network>`.
- **A payment's parts that wait for the one before** are kept in session storage alone, in memory, never on the disk: each part as Send kept it, and a record of the payment's parts, how many went and why the rest stopped if they did (`seedelf.waiting.<network>`). A lock, a closed browser or an update wipes them, and the rest never go.
- **Sealing hides what a record says, not that it exists.** Each record's name is readable (`lovejoin.mainnet` there means Lovejoin was used on mainnet), though its size doesn't count boxes or entries: each is padded to 1 KiB, or the next power of two, before sealing. A vault records no creation time; one made by a test build from before release 1.0.0 keeps it until its password is next changed; and ADA's price and when it was read stay in session storage.
- **The language** is kept unsealed (`seedelf.language`), beside the network and the account, because the screens before the unlock are shown in it: anyone reading the profile's storage sees which language the wallet is in. Every language ships inside the extension, so choosing one asks no one anything.
- **Remove wallet** deletes the vault, every sealed record but two (below), the settings, the pool list, the governance actions and any price kept on disk; so does Forgot password. Where the wallet opens, which network it's on, *Read Cardano through Koios only* and the language stay: they're the browser's, and the restore after it asks only the server that switch chose (the switch is written back after the reset when it's on, every other setting at its default). So does which public account it was on (`seedelf.account`), until a wallet is next made or restored here, which starts on account 0. It isn't a secure erase: the browser's storage may keep old copies on the disk.
  - **It first lists what's still open** on each network: a payment that may still go through; private sessions still open, or with something left behind at their accounts (a restore's scan finds what those hold, but not what each was for, nor what a site's session left open at the site, which Remove wallet's note says to close there first); a Lovejoin chain being sent; and a mix from the public account stopped at a transaction that may have gone through. Then it needs *Remove it anyway, leaving that behind* as well as the typed confirmation.
  - **It keeps a payment that may still go through** (`maybeSent.<network>`), and so does Forgot password: sealed under the phrase's key, the record stays until a wallet is next made or restored in this browser. Restoring the same phrase watches it again, and holds new payments here back until it settles; any other phrase's wallet deletes it. Nothing watches it in between, and its name shows which network it's on.
  - **It keeps a mix from the public account that may have gone through** (`lovejoinMaybe.<network>`): what that transaction spends and its ID, sealed the same way, until a wallet is next made or restored here. Restoring the same phrase looks for it before another mix from the account is built, and deletes it once settled; any other phrase's wallet deletes it. Only Remove wallet, unlocked, writes it: Forgot password can't read the Lovejoin record, so it adds none.
  - **Two times stay in memory** until the browser closes, through a lock and Remove wallet too: when the wallet last sent something, and when the browser's session began, and nothing of what was sent (`seedelf.sends`, for Lovejoin's quiet rule, *Timing* under *Lovejoin*).
- **Nothing typed is spell-checked:** with Chrome's enhanced spell check on, what's typed would go to Google, a contact's name among it.
- **Chrome's Translate is left on, and disclosed:** in a language the wallet doesn't speak, Chrome may offer to translate its page, or translate it unasked after *Always translate*, and that sends the page's text to Google, balances, addresses and Seedelf names among it. The recovery phrase's words are kept out, shown or typed (with the words suggested as you type them): they're marked not to translate, so Translate neither sends them nor changes them. Using the wallet in English, Spanish or Japanese avoids it all. The privacy policy says so.
- **Copying** an address, an ID, a Seedelf's name or a transaction's raw bytes (its view's *Raw CBOR* tab) puts it where the wallet can't reach: the system's clipboard history, and other devices where the clipboard syncs. The wallet doesn't clear the clipboard on a timer, which wouldn't reach those, and could wipe something copied since. The recovery phrase has no Copy button, and pasting it into Restore or Check recovery phrase empties the clipboard, though not its history. Selected and copied by hand, it goes onto the clipboard like any text. Pasting a private transaction's bytes into a site, an online decoder say, tells that site which contract UTxOs are yours, even for a review never sent. A swap Minswap built that failed when Minswap ran it keeps that transaction under *Details*, with Copy, for a report to Minswap; a line beside Copy says that whoever it's sent to can tie the swap's one-time account, and the private UTxOs that funded it, to you.
- **The UTxOs screen** lists the private UTxOs by outpoint, from the last reading, each tagged with where it came from. It says that looking one up on an explorer tells that site which UTxO you care about.
- **What the wallet can't prevent:** the Chrome Web Store and Chrome know the extension is installed, and Chrome Sync, if it syncs extensions, installs it in your other signed-in browsers (never the wallet itself). While the wallet is unlocked, anyone at the browser sees its balances and history, and someone who knows Chrome's developer tools can read the unlocked session's storage, keys included. The system may write memory to disk.

### The public account's collateral

- **It's only ever put up by what the account signs anyway** (a Seedelf the account pays for). Private spends never use it: giveme.my lends its own (rule 2), so no collateral of yours tags a private spend. Setting one by payment is a 5 ₳ payment from the account to itself, in the open.

### Staking and voting

- **Staking and voting are the public account's, and public,** as in any wallet: the pool, the vote delegation, the rewards and every withdrawal name the account's stake key. The Staking page, the vote page and each review say so.
- **Private money can't be staked.** It has no staking part, so it earns nothing while it's private, and no stake key links your Seedelfs. That's the cost of keeping them unlinked. The Staking page says so. Money you're just holding can stay staked in the public account until you want to move it privately.
- **Rewards spent along with a payment** (on by default, a Settings switch) add a withdrawal to a send, a Make private or a Seedelf the account pays for. It names the stake key, which the account's base addresses carry anyway, so it links nothing new. Make private with rewards moves them into the private balance with the rest.
- **What the server learns:** every balance reading asks for the account's `account_info`, and its pool's `pool_info` once while the wallet stays unlocked.
  - A pool's page asks for its `pool_info` and its `pool_history`, which tell the server which pool you're looking at, as `pool_info` alone would. Staking's **Pool details** asks for your own pool's `pool_history` only when it's unfolded: the pool every reading names.
  - **Rewards history** asks for the stake key's `account_reward_history` each time it opens, and only then: the same key every reading names, so it tells the server nothing the chain doesn't, only that the history was looked at. Nothing of it is kept, and its pools are named from the device, never asked about. **Delegation history** is the same: the key's `account_update_history`, then `tx_info` for the transactions it lists, which the server has just named, each time it opens and only then.
  - The pool list is the same for everyone, and kept on the device for a day. Searching DReps asks no one: the list of named DReps ships with the wallet. Picking one reads it from the server, which then knows which one you're considering, as delegating to it will tell everyone.
- **Nothing from anywhere else:** a DRep's metadata is read through the server, its name only. Its image, which could be on any site, is never fetched.

### Your own DRep

- **The public account as its own DRep** is public, as staking is, and the screens and every review say so. The DRep key is CIP-105's `3/0` under the account, the one Lace derives, so the same phrase is the same DRep in any wallet.
- **The DRep is the account.** Its registration, its updates and every vote are paid for from the account's UTxOs, so the chain ties the DRep, and how it votes, to the account in the open. Delegating the account's own vote to it (on by default when registering, a switch) ties the stake key to it too, which the fee's inputs had done already. Every vote is public and permanent: one replaced before voting closes stays on chain too.
- **A profile is your choice, and off by default.** None is needed to vote. With one, the wallet writes the CIP-119 file and its hash, and you publish it; the wallet never fetches it, so no new host is reached. Koios, explorers and tools like GovTool will, and anyone can read it. "Ask DRep directories not to list it" (CIP-119's `doNotList`) is on by default: the profile still reads wherever the DRep is looked up.
- **Private money carries no voting power.** It has no stake key, so what's made private stops counting toward the DRep's, and the DRep card says so.
- **What the server learns:** opening Staking asks `drep_info` about the account's DRep ID, and `epoch_params` for what registering costs, or `drep_metadata` for whether a profile matched its hash. Asking about an ID nobody has registered tells the server nothing the chain will: once it's registered, the chain ties it to the account anyway.
  - The governance actions are the same for everyone, and kept on the device for an hour (`proposal_list`); a DRep's own votes on them are one `vote_list`. Their titles and summaries are the server's reading of each action's anchor: the wallet fetches no anchor, and shows a hash mismatch the server found as a warning.
  - **Read its full text** is a link you open in a tab, like the explorer's, for an IPFS text only (`ipfs://`, or another gateway's address for a CID): through the IPFS gateway Blockfrost runs, the one an NFT's image comes through, and the line under it says Blockfrost sees the IP address ask for it, and that the browser keeps it in its history. Any other address is a host the proposer chose: it's only offered to copy, as an NFT's image on another server is.

### A note on a public send

- **A note is CIP-20's message on the transaction:** anyone can read it, for good. The form says so, and, when a recipient is a Seedelf, that the note could say whose Seedelf is paid (the payment itself doesn't).
- There's no note on the private side's flows: it would be public words on a private payment.
- Notes from others show in the public Activity as text, and Save as CSV never lets one run as a spreadsheet formula.

### ADA's price

- **It comes from CoinGecko's public API, on mainnet only:** one request for every currency, when Home or the swap form opens, at most every five minutes, and none with the currency set to *Nothing*. The currency is US dollars by default (rule 7).
- It says nothing about the wallet: CoinGecko learns only that someone at this IP address uses it. Only ADA is priced; asking about the tokens held would say what the wallet holds.

### An NFT's image

- **It's shown only when you ask,** one NFT at a time, from its details: nothing is fetched for a wallet that merely holds NFTs, or for opening Tokens. You click to show it, and this browser fetches it, with no image host or proxy of the wallet's in between.
- **A click costs two requests:** the server's `asset_info` for that token's metadata (CIP-25's and CIP-68's, two columns), and, when the image is on IPFS, one fetch from Blockfrost's gateway (`ipfs.blockfrost.dev`, the one Lace uses). Each sees this IP address ask about this NFT. An image written on chain in the metadata needs the server's request alone.
- **On the private side it's a real link,** and the details say so before the click: the chain shows which contract UTxO holds the NFT, so the server or the gateway, either alone, could tie this IP address to that part of the private balance. On the public side the server already reads the account; the gateway is the new party.
- **Only IPFS is fetched.** An image on any other server is given as its address, to copy, never opened: anyone can send the wallet an NFT, and one whose image sits on a server its sender chose would tell that server the IP address of whoever looks. A gateway's address in the metadata (`https://ipfs.io/ipfs/…`) is the same file under its CID, so it comes from Blockfrost's gateway instead.
- **Chrome's grant comes first.** The gateway sends no CORS headers, so the worker reads it through Chrome's access to that one host, which the first Show image asks Chrome for: it's part of the optional access to sites the dApp connector declares, so installing asks for nothing new. When the access given for connecting sites (`https://*/*`, never handed back) covers it already, Chrome answers with no dialog, and the details leave out that it asks. Turned down, nothing is asked of anyone, the server included. Without the grant a request would still reach the gateway and only its answer would be blocked, which is why the worker checks before it fetches.
- **Nothing is kept on the disk.** The fetch goes without cookies or a referrer, and with `cache: "no-store"`, so Chrome's cache never records which NFTs this wallet looked at. The image comes to the page as data, and stays in that window's memory until the wallet locks. A Seedelf's own token is never asked about: it has no image, and asking would tie this IP address to that Seedelf.

### An ADA Handle in the private balance

- **It isn't private, and it isn't safe:** whoever pays `$name` from another wallet pays the address holding it, the Seedelf contract, with no register, and the contract lets such a UTxO go to anyone. The wallet warns before a handle goes into the private balance, and while one sits there.

### Connecting a site

- **The connect window chooses nothing for you.** It says what each option costs, the public account (the site sees its addresses, balance and UTxOs, and keeps what it saw) or a private session (its fees, its 5 ₳ of collateral and a minute's wait), and Connect waits for a choice. There's no setting for which one new sites start on.
- **On the public account,** the site sees its addresses, its balance and its UTxOs, and everything you sign for it. That's what any wallet's CIP-30 gives. It can recognize this browser later, even when you connect it to a private session then, and the window says so.
- **The site learns nothing about Seedelf** from the connector: no Seedelf UTxO, register or key is offered, and a transaction it asks to sign is read against its own account only. For the prompt, the worker also matches what it pays against the wallet's other accounts, asking no one, and never refuses for it (below).
- **Nor about the wallet's other accounts:** a signature is checked only against its own account's Lovejoin chain, and among the wallet's recent sends a site finds only the outputs that pay its own account (so it can build on them before they land). Anything else it asks about goes through its lookup budget and the Cardano server, as a stranger's does, and gets the stranger's answer.
- **But a site can tell which Lovejoin pools this device knows:** a transaction it asks to sign that pays into a pool's reward account is refused unasked where the device knows that pool, and opens the window where it doesn't. So, for a pool newer than the list the wallet ships with, the site learns whether this device has read Lovejoin's pools since that pool started: a hint that it uses Lovejoin.
- **A payment into Seedelf Wallet's contract** in a site's transaction is shown: under a register only its owner can spend (a Seedelf payment, from the account in the open); without one, or under one anyone can spend (anyone can take it, and the window warns); or under one no one can ever spend (it's locked for good, and the window warns). One that keeps its datum by hash is none of these: no Seedelf Wallet finds it, and the window and Transaction details warn. One that carries a reference script beside its register is its owner's, but Seedelf Wallet can't spend it yet (the CLI's `sweep --all` can), and the window and Transaction details say so. Which a register is, is the contract's to say ([architecture.md](architecture.md#reading-a-transaction)).
  - **The register is the site's to write,** so the window never says such a payment hides whose Seedelf it is. The site always knows, and a register copied as it's found on chain, beside a Seedelf's token or one of your own UTxOs, shows anyone whose Seedelf it pays, and for your own, ties it to the account it's paid from. The window warns where this device's copy of the contract holds that register, asking no one; it can't see every copy.
- **A payment into your own Seedelf** in a site's transaction you sign is noted in the sealed Seedelf history as you sign it, since the site may send the transaction itself: which of its outputs are yours, and the public account or private session that signed. Once a reading finds it, Private activity lists it as *Made private* (or *Back from a private session*), money made private from that account, never a payment received (*Co-spending*); one that never lands is dropped after 30 days. Nothing is asked of anyone for it.
- **Locked or not:** a connected site's `isEnabled()` says only that it's connected, whether the wallet is locked or not, as long as the worker has read the connected sites since it started: one restarted while locked says false until the unlock. While it's locked, a site's reads are refused at once, in the words a stranger hears, and open no window; `enable()`, signatures and submits open the unlock window, which names the sites waiting. Once that window is closed, the sites that were waiting hear the words a site that isn't connected hears, for a while (10 s, then longer for each close in a row), never that the wallet is locked. A connected site can still tell locked from unlocked by whether its reads are answered, and a call the wallet locks during can still fail, saying only that the wallet couldn't answer.
- **Governance (CIP-95)** is asked for by the site and agreed to by you, never given otherwise: the connect window (or, for a site connected already, a question of its own) says what it lets the site do before you agree. On the connect window it's a switch, **off by default**: connecting a site that asks for it still hands over nothing of the DRep key unless you turn it on. Declined, it isn't asked again until the site connects anew, and the site stays connected.
  - With it, the site learns the dApp account's DRep public key and whether its stake key is registered (one `account_info`), and can ask the DRep key to sign: the account's own DRep certificates, its votes, messages as the DRep, a required signer that names it, and an input or collateral at an address paid to its hash, which its witness spends. Each one is shown first, and its privacy note says the DRep and every vote are public and tied to the account.
  - **A site without it is told nothing of the DRep key:** WebAssembly treats that key as anyone's for it, so a transaction naming it reads as needing someone else's signature, and a message for it as no key of the account's.
  - A private session has no DRep, so a site on one gets no governance, and the window says so. Settings' list of connected sites shows which have it; disconnecting takes it away.
- **Which sites are connected** is a sealed private record, like Contacts, with each page's title as it asked to connect, which the lists show after the address. The site's origin comes from Chrome, not the page. Disconnecting a site means it has to ask again before it sees anything more; it keeps what it already saw, and an open page of it may still show what it read until it's reloaded, which the wallet says, since CIP-30 has no way to tell the page.
- **The sites you declined**, and those whose page left before you answered its connect question or its wait for the unlock (a reload, say, unless another of its pages is asking to connect too), are kept in session storage, which is in memory, gone when the browser closes, and kept from content scripts: their origins, waits and counts, never a page's title. A lock keeps them, and nothing clears one when its wait ends: it stays until the browser closes, or until the list is next written (another decline, say) 10 minutes or more after its wait. They're shown only while still waiting, and only while the wallet is unlocked.
- **What the server learns:** reading the account for a site is the same query as a balance reading (at most every 30 s). Signing a site's transaction that spends UTxOs the account doesn't hold asks the server about those (`utxo_info`); they're the site's, and the transaction names them anyway.
- **Turning it on** asks Chrome to let the wallet onto every https site, and onto pages served from this computer (localhost, 127.0.0.1), for sites in development, which is what adds `window.cardano.seedelf`. The wallet doesn't read or change the pages beyond that one entry.
  - A site hears the wallet's refusals and errors in English whatever language it's set to, so a site doesn't learn its language.
  - Pages already open get it as the switch is turned on, not at their next load, so a page left open could tell roughly when that was; the switch says so, before it's turned on and after. A page that already has the entry is left alone.
  - **Every https site you open, and every page served from this computer, and the scripts on them, can then see that you use Seedelf Wallet,** even one you never connect (not your addresses or balance until you connect it), as with any CIP-30 wallet. Settings says so of https sites. With the connector off, the default, no site can tell the wallet is installed.
- **A site sees the browser:** no CIP-30 wallet can stop a site's own records, its IP address, cookies, logins, the browser's fingerprint, and the scripts in its top frame, or a tracker it shares with a site that saw the public account. A separate Chrome profile keeps cookies apart, and a VPN hides the IP address. Neither does both alone.
  - Restoring the phrase in another profile skips the sessions' accounts already used: the wallet asks the chain about a session's account at Review, and again just before its funding is sent. Two profiles funding sessions at about the same time, before either lands, can still share one.
  - Each profile reads the other's private transactions as money in, often as payments received, so its selection can't keep the two apart (*Co-spending*).
  - Each brings back the other's Lovejoin boxes on its own schedule, blind to the other's sends, so two can come back a minute apart from one IP address: keep the phrase unlocked in one profile at a time (Lovejoin's *Timing*).

### A private swap

A private swap runs from a private session: a one-time account (`24301'/0/i`) is funded from the private balance, Minswap's aggregator builds the swap for it, and everything comes back into the private balance.

- **What links, on chain:** the funding spend's private UTxOs and change to the one-time account, as Make public does; the account to the order and the proceeds; the return to the private UTxO the funding left, as Make private does. Anyone can follow the whole path. The public account isn't in any of its transactions, but money you made private yourself leads on to it through the funding (*Co-spending*).
- **The funding's shape marks it:** a spend of the Seedelf contract with giveme.my's collateral, paying an amount and exactly 5 ₳ to one new address, whose stake key the wallet never registers.
  - Too much for one transaction (a token spread over more UTxOs than one spends, sold privately), a swap's, a site's or a new mix's funding goes as several to that address, the 5 ₳ in the first, each tying what it spends to the account, as one would. A part with no ADA of its own spends what the part before carried forward, and waits for it to be in a block (*A payment in parts*).
  - So anyone watching can tell a Seedelf Wallet session as soon as it lands, before its account places an order or connects; everything after links to it anyway.
  - A private Bootstrap's funding pays the amount alone, no 5 ₳ (its deposit runs no script), so it's told apart from the other sessions' fundings. That costs it nothing, since a Bootstrap hides nothing and its deposit shows what it is, but it leaves the crowd of fundings that look alike by one.
  - A session hides among Seedelf's users, not all Cardano wallets. The 5 ₳ stays exact: it matches common collateral, where a random amount would mark Seedelf alone.
- **The return merges into the funding's change** while that's still in the private balance, so the session leaves one private UTxO tied to it, not two. When it's been spent, the return makes new ones, which are then tied to the session too.
- The amounts and the times tie the two ends together, as the path does anyway. The account's stake key is its own, used once, so it ties the session to nothing else.
- **A swap against Danogo's pools** (Danogo is a Cardano exchange) has no order: the account swaps with the pools in one transaction, which carries Minswap's own collateral, signed by Minswap, beside the account's signature. That collateral is Minswap's and in many people's swaps, so it ties the account to Minswap's aggregator, as its note on the transaction already does, and to nothing of yours. Reading the swap before the account signs it (whether the wallet runs the swap for you, or you choose *Review it myself*) asks the Cardano server about the pools' UTxOs and that collateral (`utxo_info`): neither names the account, and the swap, once sent, names both.
- **Minswap** sees the account's address, the tokens and amounts, what's searched for in its token list, and the IP address. Its note on the order (CIP-20 metadata, which anyone can read) names the account's address too.
  - **Its IP address lets it group** every swap made from that address, those running together and those after, and tie them to anything else done at Minswap from it (its own site with your public account, say); a VPN hides the address.
  - The token picker searches the wallet's own list on the device first, and asks Minswap only when nothing there matches, or on *Search Minswap*.
  - **Half and Max** are worked out from what the private balance holds, so they round down to a whole ₳ or token unit (what's left stays in the private balance), and the form says they tell Minswap roughly how much it holds.
- **The Cardano server** is asked about every open session's account together (one request, when the dApps page opens or is refreshed, and when the Swaps screen reads the chain), so it can tie them to each other and to the IP address. A swap's orders are read from it too, never from Minswap: the orders themselves, and, for a Splash order filled in part, what's at its address and the transactions that put it there (`tx_info`, each asked once). Placing an order also asks it for the protocol parameters and, the first time the worker places one through a DEX, for that DEX's order script (its reference UTxO, the same for everyone), so the wallet can run each order's cancel before it signs. Neither names the account, and the server is sent the swap itself moments later.
- **A new session's index** is checked with the server first (`account_addresses`), in case a restore or another browser used it: that one stake address alone, which its funding puts on chain anyway. Only when it was used does the wallet ask about the next 20 together, which tells the server those are one wallet's, the used ones and those to come. After a restore, or Find leftovers, the restore scan's batches have named the next ones already (*The restore scan*). Reviews that aren't sent ask about the same index again, so the server can tie them to the session that's sent at it, but to no other. Send asks once more: about that stake address, and, if it shows nothing, the account's payment key (`credential_utxos`), both of which its funding puts on chain anyway. Neither sees a mempool, so two browsers with the phrase funding sessions at about the same time can still share an account.
- **On this device:** the list of sessions (their indexes, stages and transactions, and what those paid each account) is a sealed private record, like Contacts. It also says, per network, whether a restore's scan has finished there, so right after a restore both networks' records exist, whatever was used, and their readable names (`sessions.mainnet`, `sessions.preprod`) show that the wallet was restored. A session the restore scan found that ends with nothing sent for it from here and nothing left at its account (another browser emptied it, say) leaves the list, its record with it.
- Each account is used for one session, then never again (rule 6), but for that race between two browsers. Its index comes from the phrase in order, so the restore scan finds what one-time accounts still hold after a restore, or in another browser from Find leftovers (*The restore scan*).

### A site connected to a private session

The same one-time account as a swap's, funded from the private balance in the connector's window, and used by the site as an ordinary wallet.

- **What links, on chain:** the funding to the account, as for a swap; whatever the site does with the account, in the open; each top-up; and the return to new private UTxOs. Anyone, the site included, can follow the money back into the private balance, and money you made private yourself leads on to the public account. The funding's review names the change it leaves in the private balance, which the site can follow too.
- **Your public accounts aren't in these transactions,** unless the site builds one that:
  - pays one of them or spends from one, an address paid to the hash of one of their stake or DRep keys included, which that key's witness spends (though the prompt names its output "An address": the wallet never reads it);
  - pays or spends from someone else's address, or a contract's, that carries one's stake key: any the wallet knows, the dApp account included, not only the one on screen;
  - or carries a certificate, a withdrawal or a proposal that names that account's stake key.
- **Some of those need nothing of that account's:** an old-style stake registration needs no signature of its key, so the session's alone completes it; nor does a proposal's deposit, which goes back to the reward account it names when the action ends, or a treasury withdrawal it proposes, which pays the reward accounts it names; and a stake pool's registration that makes it the pool's reward account needs only the pool's own signatures, which a site that runs the pool adds after the wallet's.
- **What the prompt can tell:**
  - It names "Your public account" (by number and name, with several) only for an output one of its payment keys spends: its first 20 on each chain (and, for the account on screen, any past them its last balance reading found) at any address, and its keys past those, up to the 500th on each chain, only under that account's own stake key.
  - Such a key with no staking part, or under a stranger's stake key, isn't recognised, so the prompt can say your public account isn't in it; under another of your accounts' stake keys it reads as someone else's address.
  - Nor is one of your keys' hashes matched where a stake key goes (a public account's payment or DRep key named as a reward account, a certificate's credential or an address's staking part), nor a committee update's member or a committee hot key's authorisation, so the prompt can say your public account isn't in it then too.
  - For an address that only carries its stake key, it says apart that the money isn't yours. A certificate's, a withdrawal's or a proposal's line says whose stake key it is ("A certificate for the stake key of your public account"; "Its … ₳ deposit comes back to the stake key of your public account when the action ends"), and a stake pool's registration keeps its own line, with the warning.
  - When the wallet couldn't check (its record of the accounts or of the sessions unreadable), the line still says the stake key or the reward account "isn't yours", and the prompt doesn't say the other side isn't in it.
  - Each way it warns that signing ties it to the session on chain, and it says the other side isn't in it only when it checked and found none of these.
- **The site** is given the account's address, its reward address (its own stake key, which the wallet never registers), its UTxOs and collateral, and what you sign for it. A funding in parts is the site's only once every part has landed: until then every call from it waits, and short of a part it's refused and its link to the session dropped, so it's never given part of one. It sees the same account on every visit until the session ends, so its visits link to each other. It also sees this browser: if it has seen your public account here, it can tell the session is yours (*Connecting a site*).
- **On this device:** which site has which session is part of the sealed list of connected sites. Once a site's session is disconnected, its record goes, and with it the site's origin. One with something left behind still at its account when it's disconnected, as the server lists it then, stays, closed, to point to it, with the site's origin and its transactions, its left-behind list cut to what's still there.

### Lovejoin

Lovejoin is a mixer. Each pool holds boxes of one value: ADA alone, or a set amount of one token and of ADA, and anyone can start a pool. A box goes in from one place and comes out, after other people's mixes, among other people's boxes; how many, and from whom that hides it, is just below. The wallet mixes only in pools on Lovejoin v2's contracts (Lovejoin `29573c5`): a private session's spare ADA and its 5 ₳ collateral go into v2's pool of 5 ₳ boxes, and the classic pool of 10 ₳ boxes and every pool on Lovejoin's v1 contracts (`8131b1d`) take no new box (*The retired pools*, below). It runs on preprod and on mainnet.

- **It has had no third-party audit,** only its makers' own review, as Lovejoin's own README and SECURITY.md say. The wallet says so where Lovejoin is chosen: Settings, the Lovejoin tile and its reviews, a swap's approval (an ADA→token swap's too, for what a stop or a refund would send through it), a return's review, and Bring everything back's review.
- **What it hides, and from whom:** which box coming out is yours, from people reading the chain, not from the Cardano server or giveme.my, which see your device send both ends (*Network*), nor from whoever put a box in under your key and mixed it themselves (below). Settings, the tile and each review say so. At best a box is one of the fan-out's `3^depth` leaves (up to 9 at the default depth 2), or `4^depth` with four boxes a mix (a Settings option: up to 16; where four can't go, the chain goes three and its review says why), and usually fewer:
  - **A box's way out shows where it goes.** The wallet brings each box back into the Seedelf contract under a register, one box a transaction, paid from the box itself with giveme.my's collateral; Lovejoin's own app writes no datum. So a box hides only among the fan-out's leaves whose owners also bring them back into a Seedelf around the same time, not among all `3^depth` leaves, nor among the 30 boxes a pool must hold before the wallet mixes there (the pool floor, below). While few people do that, a box coming back a few hours after its mixes can be picked out: for a session's return, that ties it to the session; for a mix from the public account, to the account. The public mix's review says so.
  - The session, or the public account, pays every mix and backs them with its collateral, in the open, so its leaves are public.
  - The fix would be in the ecosystem: Lovejoin's own app writing a fresh register when it withdraws to a Seedelf, so both apps' exits look alike.
- **What still links:** the deposit, to whatever paid it (a session, or the public account in the open), and the boxes going in. Nothing on a box's way back names a session or an account, but where other money pays for it (*A pool whose boxes can't pay their own way back*, below).
- **Whoever deposits a box under your key knows it's yours,** as a payer knows a payment: anyone can, from a Seedelf's register on chain. Mix my boxes again leaves such a box out (*Mixing the boxes again*, below), but not once they've mixed it themselves: it then reads as your own mixed box, so they can still follow it, into Mix my boxes again, back into your private balance and on to whatever it pays for.
- **It's on by default for private sessions' returns, and can be turned off:** Settings' *Bring private sessions back through Lovejoin*. Off, a session's ADA comes back directly, tied on chain to the session and, through its funding, to the private UTxOs that paid for it; it saves each box's mixes (about 3.3 ₳ a box at 2 waves on mainnet), about 0.3 ₳ to bring it back, and the hours of waiting. A mix from the Lovejoin tile still mixes. And each flow can come back directly on its own: a swap's approval (*Bring it back through Lovejoin*, kept with the swap), its Stop (*Stop and bring it back directly*), each return's review (its own switch, *Bring it back through Lovejoin*, first on it), and Bring everything back's review (its switch, *Bring them back through Lovejoin*).
- **A box hides only among other people's.** On mainnet the wallet draws from a pool only once it holds 30 real boxes that aren't the wallet's (the pool floor); below it a return comes back directly, and says why. So does a return while the wallet knows of no v2 pool of 5 ₳ boxes, saying the wallet knew of none then, but for its tokens, which still go into their own pools; once such a pool exists, the wallet uses it. A swap's approval reads the pool at Review (kept for five minutes), counts only the boxes it has room for, and says when the ADA would come back directly. 30 boxes means enough to mix with, not 30-way hiding: the floor counts boxes, not owners, however long they've sat, and boxes seeded by the pool's operator alone would hide nothing from the operator.
- **Bootstrap** (depth 0, no mixes, from either side) is what starts an empty pool, since a mix needs others' boxes and the wallet's own never count. It hides nothing of whoever puts it in, which its review says in a warning.
  - Its boxes stay in the pool for other people to mix with, and never come back by themselves. Some pools still hold a seed, which is like a Bootstrap but comes back by itself after the delay set in Settings; the wallet makes Bootstraps, not seeds. In a retired pool nothing is held, and they come back by themselves (*The retired pools*).
  - They come back by hand: one at a time, which asks first, since a box nobody has mixed since shows its way in and out, and brings back one someone else's mix has moved first; or all of them, which lets them come back by themselves from then on, one a run, at random delays, never in one transaction.
  - Each box brought back by hand counts against the Bootstrap that held it: the one whose deposit put it there, or, for one someone else's mix moved, the oldest still missing a box at its deposit. It happens at a pool read when more boxes could come back than the wallet has due times for (after a restore, a box from another device or one someone else deposited under your key, or after Mix my boxes again took boxes not mixed yet), asking about all such boxes together, which groups them as one person's before any is withdrawn or mixed again. And it happens in any wallet wherever a box of unknown origin is taken (a box someone else's mix moved is one): Mix my boxes again asks about all of them together (Pay from my private balance anyway asks nothing); Bring one back now, and Bring one back in a pool whose boxes can't pay their own way back, about those ranked ahead of the first known one, and a Bootstrap's Bring one back about the box it picks; and a box coming back by itself alone, just before its withdraw, which then shows Koios that box anyway if it goes, but in a retired pool, where it isn't asked: with no record or answer, it comes back tagged Unknown too, as one Bring every box back now takes does, which asks nothing, or Bring one back anyway, which puts no box in the ask. Each transaction is asked about once and the answer kept; one Koios doesn't answer for at a pool read, or in the once-more ask below, is asked again at every later pool read that counts the boxes until it does, Bring one back anyway's too, which takes such a box first where there is one; one it doesn't answer for when only a take asked (Mix my boxes again, a Bring one back press's boxes, a box just before its own withdraw) isn't kept, and is asked again at the next such take (a due box waits a fresh 5 to 60 minutes). While a box is held for that answer, with no due time, each such read asks about the pool's other boxes no record or answer names too, together. An answer an earlier version kept that names no public account was kept without what the transaction spent: where it says a deposit made a box still in a current pool, which can't be told the phrase's own or someone else's without that, or where the wallet knows an account past the first 24, which it may have missed, that transaction is asked about once more, its boxes in a current pool held meanwhile, and with it, where a deposit made the box, the pool's other boxes no record or answer names, in the same request.
- **On this device:** the boxes' due times and each chain the wallet sends (its deposit, its mixes and its leaves, and for a mix from the public side the account that paid it, kept while their boxes sit unmoved; once its chain's record goes, which of its leaves a mix made, and, for a deposit no mix followed, the public account or private session that paid it, kept by the deposit itself), the pools it started, with each one's values, and each mix's count of its boxes back, kept until they all are, sealed per network, with what the Cardano server said made each box no record accounts for (a mix, a deposit, which public account's; where it named none, the payment and stake keys of what that transaction spent, and which accounts they were checked against) and the transactions it hasn't answered for yet that the pool reads ask about again (above), each kept until 3 hours after a pool read last listed a box of it; and, for a wallet that had something in the retired pools, when it retired them. The boxes themselves are found again by the Seedelf key.

### Bring everything back

- **It returns each session in its own transaction,** so no transaction ties two sessions together.
- They're sent one after another, though: returns into Seedelf that land within a block or two of each other hint that their one-time accounts are one person's. With Lovejoin, the sessions' deposits land in the same block or two and their mixes share blocks, and a shallow pool serves the first sessions while the rest come back directly.
- Bringing each back from its own page, hours apart, avoids both. The review opens with a switch, *Bring them back through Lovejoin*, to bring them back directly instead, and says Lovejoin has had no audit. The wallet doesn't spread them out by itself.

### Preprod and mainnet share keys

- **A recovery phrase has the same payment keys, stake key and Seedelf key on both networks;** only the addresses' prefix differs. So anyone comparing the two chains can tell a preprod account is the same person's mainnet one.
- **So do private sessions' one-time accounts:** each network counts its sessions from 0, and a new session's index is checked only on the network in use, so a preprod session and a mainnet one at the same index share their keys, and anyone comparing the chains can tie them together. This is a known limit, not fixed.
- Settings says so before moving to preprod, and preprod's note says to test with a recovery phrase you don't use on mainnet.
- **What's kept per network stays apart:** balances, the Seedelf history, sessions, Lovejoin, connected sites and the rest. A site connected on one network isn't on the other.
- **The choice itself** (`seedelf.network`) is kept unsealed in local storage, like where the wallet opens: it says only which network the wallet is on.

### Save as CSV

- **It writes the listed Activity to a file on the device, unencrypted.** For the private side that's more than the payments: each row has its transaction's ID, so whoever has the file can find every one on the chain. It ties the private payments, the private sessions and the Lovejoin boxes to each other and to the public account, and shows which Seedelf each payment went to.
- The screen says so, and to give it only to someone you'd show all of that. There's no export without IDs: amounts, fees and times identify the transactions anyway.
- **The public side's file names your own Make private and Make public, as the screen does:** its *Made private* and *Made public* rows tell whoever has it that the money went into or came out of your private balance, where the chain shows only a payment into or out of the wallet contract, as for someone else's Seedelf. The names stay, since the file is for accounting, and the note under Save as CSV says what they show. A public Send, or a site's transaction you signed, is written as the chain reads it, a payment, even where it paid one of your own Seedelfs.
